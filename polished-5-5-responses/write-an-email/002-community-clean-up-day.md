# Community Clean-Up Day

## Prompt
Your neighborhood association is organizing a community clean-up day. Write an email to your neighbors inviting them to participate and explaining the details.

## My Draft
Hello everyone,

Our community will have clean up day this Saturday. Please join us if you have time. We need many people to help clean the park and the street. Bring your own gloves if possible. See you there!

## Polished Response (final — for review & teaching)
Hi neighbors,

I'm writing to invite you to our community clean-up day this Saturday, starting at 9 AM at the main park entrance. We'll be cleaning up the park and surrounding streets, and the more hands we have, the faster and more thorough the job will be.

If you have your own gardening gloves, please bring them along; we'll have a limited number of spares for anyone who needs one. Trash bags and other supplies will be provided.

We hope to see many of you there — it's a great chance to help keep our neighborhood looking its best while getting to know the people who live around us.

Best,
The Community Association

## What Changed & Why
- **Missing specific details:** The draft says "this Saturday" with no time or meeting point. The polished version adds "9 AM at the main park entrance," which is essential in a practical invitation email.
- **Weak call to action ("Please join us if you have time"):** The phrase "if you have time" unintentionally signals that attendance doesn't really matter. The polished version replaces it with a positive, motivating reason: "the more hands we have, the faster and more thorough the job will be."
- **Vague supply information:** The draft only mentions gloves without clarifying what else will be provided. The polished version specifies that trash bags and other supplies will be on hand, making the email more informative and reassuring.
- **No closing motivation / community benefit:** The draft ends abruptly with "See you there!" The polished version closes with a sentence highlighting both the practical benefit (keeping the neighborhood clean) and the social benefit (meeting neighbors), which makes the invitation more compelling.
- **Informal/incomplete greeting and sign-off:** "Hello everyone" and no formal sign-off are too casual for a neighborhood association communication. "Hi neighbors" and "Best, / The Community Association" strike the right semi-formal tone for this context.

## Reusable Patterns
- **"I'm writing to invite you to…"** — A clear, formal opener for any invitation email that immediately signals the email's purpose.
- **"The more [noun] we have, the [comparative adjective] the [result] will be."** — A persuasive structure for encouraging participation or action (e.g., "The more feedback we receive, the better the plan will be.").
- **"Please bring [item] along; we'll have a limited number of spares for anyone who needs one."** — A polite, practical way to make a request while reassuring readers who may not have the item.
- **"It's a great chance to… while…"** — A versatile pattern for highlighting dual benefits of one activity (e.g., "It's a great chance to practice English while making new friends.").
- **"[Supplies/materials] will be provided."** — A concise, reassuring phrase useful in any event or activity invitation to reduce barriers to participation.
