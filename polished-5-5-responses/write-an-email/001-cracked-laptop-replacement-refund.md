# Cracked Laptop Replacement or Refund

## Prompt
You recently ordered a laptop online, but when it arrived, you found that the screen was cracked. Write an email to the online store expressing your concern and asking for a replacement or refund.

## My Draft
Dear Sir or Madam,

I bought a laptop from your store last week and when it arrive the screen is cracked. I am very disappoint about this because I need it for work. I want you to send me new one or give my money back. Please reply soon.

Thank you,
Alex

## Polished Response (final — for review & teaching)
Dear Customer Service Team,

I am writing to report an issue with a laptop I purchased from your store last week (Order #48213). Upon opening the package, I discovered that the screen was cracked, which I assume occurred either during manufacturing or shipping.

Since I rely on this laptop for work, I would appreciate a prompt resolution. Specifically, I would like to request either a replacement unit or a full refund, whichever your policies allow.

Please let me know what documentation or next steps you require. I look forward to your response at your earliest convenience.

Best regards,
Alex

## What Changed & Why
- **Verb tense errors fixed:** "when it arrive" → "when it arrived" and "the screen is cracked" → "the screen was cracked"; past tense is required when describing a completed event.
- **Adjective form corrected:** "very disappoint" → the polished version avoids this entirely and replaces the sentiment with a formal tone; "disappoint" must be "disappointed" as a predicate adjective.
- **Vague request made precise:** "send me new one" → "a replacement unit"; articles ("a") and specific nouns are required in formal writing.
- **Tone lifted to formal register:** Casual phrases like "give my money back" and "Please reply soon" were replaced with "a full refund" and "I look forward to your response at your earliest convenience" — standard complaint-email conventions.
- **Context and detail added:** Including a plausible order number and a reason for the damage (manufacturing or shipping) makes the complaint more credible and actionable, which is rewarded in TOEFL writing tasks.

## Reusable Patterns
- **"I am writing to report an issue with [item] I purchased from your store on/last [time]."** — Standard complaint-email opener that immediately establishes purpose.
- **"Upon [verb+ing], I discovered that..."** — Formal way to sequence events (e.g., "Upon opening the box, I noticed that the item was damaged.").
- **"I would appreciate a prompt resolution."** — Polite but firm phrase to signal urgency without sounding rude.
- **"...whichever your policies allow."** — Flexible closing to a request; shows awareness of the recipient's constraints, which reads as professional.
- **"Please let me know what documentation or next steps you require."** — Useful in any service/complaint email to signal cooperation and move the conversation forward.
