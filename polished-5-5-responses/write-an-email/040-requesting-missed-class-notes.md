# Requesting Missed Class Notes

## Prompt
Background:
You are a student who missed a recent class. You want to ask your classmate, Jessica, for her notes and any important details from the class.

Requirements:
1. Explain why you missed the class
2. Ask for her notes and explain why you need them
3. Suggest what you could do for her in return

## My Polished Response
Hi Jessica,

I'm sorry I missed class yesterday. I had a medical appointment that couldn't be rescheduled, and I'd like to catch up before our next assignment.

Would you be willing to share your notes and let me know whether the professor announced any deadlines or project changes? Your notes would help me review the main concepts and understand the examples discussed in class. Photos would be perfectly fine if that's easiest for you.

To return the favor, I'd be happy to get you a Google AI Pro subscription or help proofread your next assignment, whichever you would find more useful.

Thanks so much for your help!

Best,
Ariel

## My Score Explained
Estimated task score: 5/5 (a coaching assessment of the written response, not an official ETS score or a CEFR section-score determination).

The email addresses all three required actions with relevant supporting details. "A medical appointment that couldn't be rescheduled" explains the absence, while catching up "before our next assignment" establishes the immediate purpose. The request covers both notes and important announcements, specifically "deadlines or project changes." The explanation about reviewing concepts and classroom examples clarifies why the notes are needed. The final body paragraph offers two concrete ways to return the favor and lets Jessica choose which would be more useful.

The organization supports the communicative purpose: explanation, request and learning need, an easy sharing method, and an offer of help in return. "Photos would be perfectly fine" reduces the effort required from the recipient and makes the request easier to fulfill. The greeting "Hi Jessica," the softened request "Would you be willing," and the closing "Thanks so much" establish a consistently friendly, polite tone appropriate for a classmate.

The language shows effective syntactic variety through a relative clause about the appointment, an embedded question beginning "whether," an "if" clause about sending photos, and the recipient-focused clause "whichever you would find more useful." Expressions such as "catch up," "return the favor," and "proofread your next assignment" are idiomatic and precise. Grammar, spelling, capitalization, and punctuation are consistently controlled, and the concise message provides enough detail to fulfill its purpose.
