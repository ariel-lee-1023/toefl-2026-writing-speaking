# Company Presentation Timeline Concerns

## Prompt
You have been selected to represent your department at a company-wide presentation next month. Your manager, Mr. Chen, chose you because of your experience with the project. However, you have some concerns about the timeline and would like to discuss the presentation topic.
Write an email to Mr. Chen. In your email, do the following:

* Explain why you are excited about the opportunity.

* Describe your concerns about the timeline.

* Propose a meeting to discuss the presentation details.

Write as much as you can and in complete sentences.

## My Polished Response
Dear Mr. Chen,

Thank you for selecting me to represent our department at next month’s company-wide presentation. I am genuinely excited about the opportunity because of the experience I have gained on this project and the chance to share our results with colleagues across the company.

At the same time, I have some concerns about the current timeline. Preparing a clear, data-driven presentation with polished visuals will require careful planning and coordination with other team members. I want to make sure we have enough time to draft and refine the material without putting our ongoing project deliverables at risk.

Would you be available for a short meeting early next week, perhaps Tuesday or Wednesday afternoon? I would like to confirm the exact topic focus, the expected length, and the key points you would like me to emphasize.

Thank you again for the opportunity. I look forward to your thoughts.

Best regards,  
Alex

## My Draft
Dear Mr. Chen, It is a great honor that you appreaciate those project and selecting me on behalf of our department at next month's company-wide presentation.
While I am eager to deliver this presentation, I have a few concerns regarding our current timeline and preparation schedule. Given that creating a polished, data-driven presentation with high-quality visual aids requires thorough planning and cross-functional coordination, I want to make sure we allocate sufficient time to draft and refine the presentation without compromising our ongoing project deliverables.
To ensure that we can align on expectations and effectively plan the presentation topic and structure, I would appreciate the opportunity to meet with you briefly early next week.

## My Diagnosis
The draft is a Score 4. It successfully covers all three required actions with adequate elaboration: enthusiasm for the opportunity, concrete concerns about timeline and preparation, and a proposal to meet. The tone is professional and the organization is clear. The ceiling is created by noticeable language and convention issues: spelling and wording errors in the opening (“appreaciate those project,” “selecting me on behalf of our department”), missing punctuation after the greeting and around the closing, and an incomplete sign-off. These problems are limited but prevent the consistent precision and fully appropriate social conventions of a Score 5 response.

## My What Changed & Why
The polished version retains the same three-part structure and professional register while raising language control and social conventions to Score 5 level. The opening is rewritten for grammatical accuracy and natural flow, correctly expressing both the honor of selection and the genuine reason for excitement (project experience and the chance to share results). The timeline concern is kept constructive and specific, emphasizing the need for coordination and quality without sounding negative. The meeting request is made more concrete by suggesting a time window and listing clear agenda items (topic focus, length, key points), which strengthens elaboration. Standard greeting and closing punctuation plus a complete sign-off restore consistent social conventions. Surface errors in spelling, articles, and word forms are removed, producing precise and idiomatic workplace language. The result satisfies all Score 5 criteria: effective elaboration supporting the communicative purpose, syntactic variety with precise word choice, consistent appropriate social conventions, and almost no lexical or grammatical errors beyond those expected under timed conditions.
