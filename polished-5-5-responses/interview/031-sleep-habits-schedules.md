# Sleep Habits Schedules

## Q1 Prompt

## Q2 Prompt

## Q3 Prompt

## Q4 Prompt
Good point. For my final question, some people believe that schools and workplaces should start later in the morning so that people can get more sleep. Do you agree with this idea? Please explain.

## Q1 My Polished Response
There are two typical activities I engage in before going to bed. First, I take a warm shower to signal to my brain that it is time to wind down and release physical tension. For instance, stepping out of a warm shower always leaves me feeling refreshed and mentally clear after a busy day. Second, once I lie down in bed, I listen to smooth classical music. This peaceful background music creates an ideal atmosphere for quiet reflection, which helps me transition smoothly into restful sleep.

## Q2 My Polished Response
I definitely sleep best when I stay up late and sleep in, primarily because I prefer completing my most demanding tasks during quiet night hours. I find that reaching a state of productive mental exhaustion late at night makes it much easier for me to fall asleep naturally. For example, working late on complex academic assignments without daytime distractions allows me to focus fully and sleep deeply once finished. Therefore, maintaining a late-night schedule maximizes both my productivity and my sleep quality.

## Q3 My Polished Response
In my opinion, using smartphones and electronic devices right before bedtime has a distinctly negative impact on overall sleep quality. First, browsing short video platforms like TikTok makes it remarkably easy for people to lose track of time and delay their sleep schedule. For instance, many individuals intend to watch videos for five minutes but end up scrolling for hours. Second, exposure to bright blue light suppresses melatonin production and excites the brain, making it difficult to fall into deep sleep.

## Q4 My Polished Response
I do not think delaying start times is an effective way to help people adopt healthier sleep patterns. The primary reason is that individual habits quickly adjust to external schedule changes rather than increasing overall sleep duration. If schools or workplaces delay their morning start times by an hour, people will simply push their bedtimes an hour later, leaving their total sleep time unchanged. Therefore, the real key to better sleep lies in personal discipline and setting consistent bedtime boundaries rather than altering institutional schedules.
