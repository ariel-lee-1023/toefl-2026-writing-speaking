## Q3 Prompt

## Q4 Prompt
Good points. One final question. Do you agree or disagree that it is much easier to make close friends when you are young than when you are an adult? Why or why not?

## Q1 My Polished Response
When I spend time with my friends, we usually meet up to play soccer together at our neighborhood park. First, playing soccer gives us an active, healthy way to get some exercise while enjoying friendly physical competition. For instance, after a busy week of sitting at our desks, running around the field helps us release stress and recharge our energy. Second, after our matches, we routinely head out to grab dinner at local restaurants to catch up on each other's busy lives. Sharing a meal allows us to unwind, talk freely, and deepen our friendships in a relaxed setting. Therefore, combining casual athletic games with dinner conversations remains our favorite way to stay connected.

## Q2 My Polished Response
When I have not seen a friend for a while, I always prefer to take the initiative and reach out first. Initiating contact demonstrates that I genuinely value our friendship, regardless of how busy our personal schedules might be. For example, since I am currently studying abroad while my close friends remain in our hometown, I proactively message them whenever an upcoming academic break approaches. By taking the lead, I can suggest specific dates and organize local activities so we can catch up without delay. Although coordinating schedules across time zones takes extra effort, taking that first step ensures our bond remains strong over time. Consequently, reaching out first is the most effective way to keep meaningful friendships alive.

## Q3 My Polished Response
I firmly believe that a good friend should tell you the hard truth kindly rather than simply protecting your feelings. While empathy and emotional comfort are essential in any healthy relationship, true friends offer honest and constructive feedback to help you grow as a person. For instance, when I was overcommitting to extracurricular projects last semester, a close friend gently pointed out that my academic grades were slipping. Although hearing that criticism hurt initially, it forced me to reprioritize my coursework and saved my GPA. While it is important to deliver tough messages with care and respect, avoiding the truth only allows problems to linger. Ultimately, honest feedback is the greatest sign of genuine care and loyalty in a friendship.

## Q4 My Polished Response
I strongly disagree with the idea that making close friends is significantly easier during childhood than in adulthood. While children have shared daily school routines, adults possess a much clearer sense of personal identity and mature values. For example, adults can easily connect with like-minded peers through established professional ambitions, specialized hobbies, and shared intellectual interests, creating immediate common ground. In contrast, childhood friendships often depend merely on geographical proximity or assigned classrooms, which can fade as people grow apart. Although adult schedules are certainly busier, the connections made in mature years are built on conscious alignment and mutual respect. Thus, forming deep and lasting friendships in adulthood is often more fulfilling and equally achievable.
