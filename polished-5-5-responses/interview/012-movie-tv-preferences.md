## Q1 My Polished Response
I would say I enjoy workplace dramas and shows about tech entrepreneurship more than any other genre. What draws me in is seeing ambitious characters tackle complex, real-world problems under intense pressure. For example, in a show like Silicon Valley, you get to watch a small team of developers build a startup from scratch, facing constant financial and technical setbacks. On top of that, the show blends that high-stakes drama with sharp satirical comedy, which keeps the narrative entertaining while still giving real insight into the tech industry. That balance of humor and smart storytelling is what makes those series stand out for me.

## Q2 My Polished Response
For me, it really depends on the genre of the film. When I watch a blockbuster or a superhero movie, I definitely prefer a happy ending because it provides a satisfying sense of hope and closure. On the other hand, when it comes to social dramas—like Ken Loach's film I, Daniel Blake—I actually prefer an open or tragic ending. That kind of ending feels far more authentic to the real-world struggles the movie is depicting, and it forces the audience to confront systemic issues long after the film finishes. So while uplifting conclusions work for pure entertainment, realistic endings are what make serious dramas truly impactful.

## Q3 My Polished Response
In my opinion, people actually enjoy movies less now that streaming has replaced the traditional cinema experience. The biggest reason is that home viewing destroys total immersion. When people watch movies on their laptops or TVs, they constantly check their phones, pause for breaks, or get distracted by household chores, which completely breaks the narrative momentum. Furthermore, even when people do visit actual movie theaters today, rowdy audience members frequently disrupt the screening by talking or checking bright screens in the dark. As a result, the deep, uninterrupted engagement that used to make film viewing so special has largely been lost.

## Q4 My Polished Response
I believe it is crucial for films to offer something meaningful, though I prefer to think of it as inspiring thought rather than outright teaching. When a movie tries to lecture the audience on what is right or wrong, it can feel heavy-handed and patronizing. Instead, the best films present complex ethical dilemmas and allow viewers to draw their own conclusions. For instance, a great historical drama or documentary does not tell you what to think; it simply presents human choices in all their complexity and encourages you to reflect on them afterward. Ultimately, movies are most powerful when they entertain us while sparking genuine intellectual curiosity.
