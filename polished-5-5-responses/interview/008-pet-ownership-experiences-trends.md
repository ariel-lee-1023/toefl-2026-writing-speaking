## Q1 My Polished Response
The most common pets in my neighborhood are undoubtedly dogs and cats. Walking through my community, you constantly see families out with their dogs, which creates a very active and sociable atmosphere. On the other hand, many residents, including myself, prefer keeping cats indoors. I have three cats at home, and because they are content staying inside, they suit quiet household environments exceptionally well.

## Q2 My Polished Response
Personally, I much prefer low-maintenance pets like cats over high-maintenance animals like dogs or fish. Cats do not require daily walks or strict outdoor routines, making them very easy to integrate into a busy schedule. At the same time, unlike fish, which offer little direct interaction, cats are affectionate and lovely companions that actively seek your attention whenever they want to play.

## Q3 My Polished Response
In my opinion, having pets at home is highly beneficial for children because it teaches them practical empathy and responsibility. Caring for another living creature helps kids look beyond their own immediate desires and understand what an animal actually needs. While parents do inherit some extra daily tasks, incorporating these responsibilities into regular family routines prevents the workload from feeling overwhelming.

## Q4 My Polished Response
I believe pet ownership will become increasingly common in big cities in the future, largely driven by changing social trends and rising income levels. Animal-related media is already among the most popular content online, showing a deep interest in pet culture among younger generations. As these young professionals gain financial stability, they are increasingly choosing to welcome pets into their urban homes as full family members.

## My What Changed & Why
Across all four questions, the response structures were aligned to the standard C–D–E–F framework (Commit, Detail, Elaborate, Finish). Vague terms like "PADS" were corrected to standard terminology like "pets" or "pet ownership," and awkward adverbs like "objectively" were replaced with clear stance markers ("undoubtedly," "personally"). Grammar errors were resolved by ensuring subject-verb agreement ("my only pet are cats" -> "I much prefer low-maintenance pets like cats") and refining sentence clauses to show contrast and cause-and-effect ("on the other hand," "because they are content staying inside," "unlike fish, which offer little direct interaction").
