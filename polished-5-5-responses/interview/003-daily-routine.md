## Q1 My Polished Response
My routine these days is actually quite structured. For example, I always wake up at 7 a.m., make some coffee, and check my to-do list. The biggest reason for this fixed schedule is my current internship at a law firm, which requires me to work from 9 a.m. to 6 p.m. To add to that, I keep my evenings completely relaxed, so after work, I just return home and watch movies or read books to unwind.

## Q2 My Polished Response
Honestly, I would design a schedule that is a mix of both structured and flexible elements. The main detail is that the core components of my day, such as work, hobbies, and sleep, must be fixed because they are essential. However, the exact order of those elements should remain entirely flexible depending on how I feel. That way, I ensure everything gets done without feeling trapped in a rigid timeline.

## Q3 My Polished Response
I completely agree that how you perform in the morning determines the rest of your day. What you do between 7 and 9 a.m. essentially sets your baseline energy level. For instance, if you just sleep through that time, you will likely feel sluggish and sleepy all day. In contrast, if you wake up early and do some real work to prepare yourself, you will carry that energetic momentum into the afternoon.

## Q4 My Polished Response
I actually disagree with the idea that a strict routine is the key to reducing stress. The real factor in achieving a relaxed state is simply making sure your activities align with your genuine interests. For example, if you do not follow a typical routine but instead choose to do your favorite activities spontaneously, that will naturally keep your stress levels low.
