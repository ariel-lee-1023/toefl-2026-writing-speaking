# Cooking Habits Future Food

## Q1 Prompt
Thank you for participating. I'd like to ask you about your cooking habits. Are there certain times of the day when you're more likely to cook something? For example, breakfast, lunch, dinner, or snacks?

## Q2 Prompt
Great. When you cook, do you prefer following a recipe or creating your own dishes? Why?

## Q3 Prompt
Interesting. Some people believe that learning to cook is an essential life skill while others think it's not necessary in today's world. Which view do you agree with and why?

## Q4 Prompt
Good points. I just have one last question. Some people believe that in the future, food will mainly be about nutrition and convenience. Others believe that flavor, tradition, and culture will remain essential. What do you think food will be like in the future, and why?

## Q1 My Polished Response
Honestly, I'm most likely to cook lunch during the day. The biggest reason is that stepping into the kitchen right after finishing my morning classes or internship work gives me a much-needed mental break. Preparing a warm meal helps me reset my focus and recharge my energy for the afternoon. On top of that, cooking lunch myself allows me to control my ingredients so I can make something healthy and flavorful, like a quick vegetable stir-fry, instead of relying on heavy takeout options. That is why lunch is my preferred time to cook.

## Q2 My Polished Response
I definitely prefer starting with a standard recipe and then adding my own personal tweaks. For me, a recipe provides a reliable foundation, especially regarding cooking times and essential ingredients, but improvising on the spot is where the fun begins. For example, when I make a classic curry dish, I will follow the instructions for the base, but I often substitute the vegetables or adjust the spice blend to match whatever fresh ingredients I have in my refrigerator. That combination of structure and spontaneity gives me the best results every time.

## Q3 My Polished Response
I strongly believe that learning to cook remains an essential life skill, even in our modern world. Granted, mobile delivery apps and local restaurants make ordering food extremely convenient nowadays. However, relying entirely on prepared meals is both expensive and unhealthier in the long run. When you know how to cook, you gain complete control over nutrition, portion sizes, and sodium levels, which is crucial for long-term physical health. Beyond that, preparing your own meals saves a significant amount of money over time, making it a fundamental skill for independent living.

## Q4 My Polished Response
In my opinion, future food will still center on flavor, tradition, and cultural identity rather than pure nutrition and convenience. It is true that technology will make functional foods and quick synthetic meals more accessible. However, food is far more than just biological fuel; it is a vital way for people to celebrate their heritage and connect socially with family and friends. People will always crave the comfort and complex flavors of traditional dishes passed down through generations. Therefore, while convenient options will expand, traditional culinary culture will remain indispensable.
