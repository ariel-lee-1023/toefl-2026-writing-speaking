# Dietary Habits

## Q1 Prompt
Scenario: As part of a university project, you have agreed to take part in a short research interview about dietary habits. A graduate student conducting the research will ask you some questions online.

Thank you for your participation. Today, I'd like to ask you some questions about your dietary habits. When you have a meal at school or work, do you prefer to eat alone, or do you like to eat with friends or coworkers? Why?

## Q2 Prompt
Thank you. And what kind of meals do your family or friends generally like to eat? For example, do they prefer traditional home cooked dishes, fast food, or other types?

## Q3 Prompt
Interesting. Next, I'd like to get your opinion. In recent years, many people have argued that plant based diets are better for individual health and for the planet. Do you think that in the future, more people will adopt plant based diets? Why or why not?

## Q4 Prompt
Good points. I just have one more question. Some people believe that having regular meal times contributes to better health and financial well being. Do you agree or disagree with this idea? Explain your answer.

## Q1 My Polished Response
I usually prefer eating with friends or coworkers because lunch is a chance to take a proper break. If I eat alone at my desk, I tend to keep checking messages, so it barely feels like I've stopped working. Sitting with other people encourages me to put my phone away and have a conversation. We might talk about a film or our weekend plans instead of the next assignment. Of course, I sometimes eat alone when our schedules don't match. But whenever possible, I enjoy sharing a meal because it makes an ordinary workday feel more social.

## Q2 My Polished Response
My family generally prefers simple home-cooked meals, like rice with vegetables and chicken or tofu. These dishes are familiar, and it's easy to adjust the ingredients to suit everyone's taste. For example, we can use whichever vegetables are already in the fridge instead of buying something for a complicated recipe. That also helps us avoid wasting food. My friends order fast food occasionally when they're busy, but many still appreciate a homemade meal when they have time. I think the main attraction is having something comforting that doesn't require a lot of money or preparation.

## Q3 My Polished Response
I think more people will eat mainly plant-based meals in the future, although many will still have meat occasionally. A gradual change is easier to maintain than giving up familiar foods all at once. For example, someone could start by choosing a tofu and vegetable dish for lunch a few times a week. If it tastes good and costs less, there's a practical reason to keep choosing it. Restaurants can help by making those meals easy to find. I expect price, taste, and convenience to matter just as much as people's concerns about health and the environment.

## Q4 My Polished Response
I generally agree that regular meal times can support both healthier habits and better control over spending. When I know roughly when I'll eat, it's easier to prepare a meal instead of grabbing whatever is available at the last minute. For example, packing lunch before a busy day means I have something planned rather than buying snacks and then ordering an expensive delivery. A routine also helps me notice when work is taking over my lunch break. The exact time doesn't need to be rigid, though. The benefit comes from planning consistently and making room for proper meals.
