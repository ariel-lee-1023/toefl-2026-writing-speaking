# Reading Habits

## Q1 Prompt
Thank you for your participation. I’d like to know about your reading habits. How do you usually get information or stay entertained? Through reading, videos, conversations with others, or another way? Why does that approach work for you?

## Q2 Prompt
Can you recall a time when reading something, whether a book, article, or even a social media post, changed your perspective or taught you something new? Please explain.

## Q3 Prompt
Do you think reading is becoming more or less common in today’s world? What are some possible reasons for that change?

## Q4 Prompt
One final question. In your opinion, how important is it to preserve older books and libraries in a digital world?

## Q1 My Polished Response
Honestly, my favorite way to get information is by reading books. The biggest reason is that reading doesn't just give you raw facts; it gives you direct access to how experts think, allowing you to follow their exact cognitive patterns. On top of that, compared to quick videos or articles, books offer a much denser and wiser structure of information. Because they are so comprehensive, they force you to slow down and really engage with the material. So for me, nothing beats a good book.

## Q2 My Polished Response
If I had to pick a specific moment that changed my view, I would bring up an article I read recently about factory workers in China who were studying German philosophy. Honestly, it gave me a profound sense of shock. I had a preconceived notion about who reads dense philosophy, but seeing everyday workers demonstrate both the deep need and the intellectual ability to understand such complexity completely shattered my stereotype. It taught me never to underestimate people's drive for intellectual growth.

## Q3 My Polished Response
I definitely think reading is becoming less common these days, for a couple of reasons. First of all, a lot of students my age just don't see reading as an enjoyable activity anymore. They associate books purely with mandatory assignments and the miserable experience of memorizing dry textbooks. So, the moment they have free time, reading is the last thing they want to do. Second, new media like short-form video can provide the exact same information much faster. If people just need specific facts quickly, they will watch a video rather than read a book.

## Q4 My Polished Response
I believe it is incredibly important to preserve older books in both physical libraries and the digital world. Even with all the convenience of digital services, the physical quality of a real book is simply more robust and permanent than a digital file that could be corrupted. Beyond that, physical libraries are essential community spaces. They aren't just storage facilities; they are important buildings where residents can go to have a genuinely quiet, almost spiritual experience. You simply can't replicate that feeling on a screen.
