# Reading Habits

## Q1 Prompt
First, what kind of books do your family or friends generally like to read? For example, do they prefer fiction, non fiction, biographies, or other types?

## Q2 Prompt
I see when you read your favorite book. Do you prefer to read it in a quiet place? Or do you like to read it where there is some background noise? WHY?

## Q3 Prompt
Next, I'd like to get your opinion. If you have time to read one novel, do you think it more worthwhile to read an old classic that many readers love? Or would you choose a newly published novel that tells a modern story? WHY?

## Q4 Prompt
I have one more question. Some people believe that reading helps improve one's imagination and creativity. Do you agree with this idea? Or do you think there are other activities that can also enhance these skills? Explain why you think so.

## Q1 My Polished Response
The most common genre my family and friends enjoy reading is historical fiction. They gravitate toward these books because they provide an immersive ancient setting and well-documented historical facts, but they are also constructed with nuanced plots and complex characters. This combination allows my friends to have deep discussions and truly imagine themselves living in that specific time period.

## Q2 My Polished Response
I definitely prefer to read with some background noise, specifically classical music, rather than in complete silence. If an environment is entirely quiet, I actually find my mind wandering, which leads to unwanted distractions. However, playing classical piano pieces, such as those by Chopin or Bach, helps me focus my thoughts and allows me to become fully immersed in the reading experience.

## Q3 My Polished Response
I would much rather choose to read an old classic than a newly published novel. First, I often read to escape the pressures of the modern world, so diving into a contemporary story defeats that purpose for me. Second, classic literature typically explores profound, universal themes that represent essential human nature. These older works established the foundational storytelling techniques that many modern novels simply attempt to imitate, making the originals far more rewarding to read.

## Q4 My Polished Response
While I agree that reading definitely enhances a person's imagination, I believe that engaging in discussions about those stories is an equally powerful way to build creativity. When people share their ideas after finishing a book or watching a movie, they are exposed to entirely new perspectives. These social interactions force individuals to continuously contrast their own interpretations with others, which actively builds their creative capacity far more than just consuming the media in isolation.
