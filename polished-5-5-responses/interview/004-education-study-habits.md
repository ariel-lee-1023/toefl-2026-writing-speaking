# Education Study Habits

## Q1 Prompt
Thank you for joining the study. I'd like to ask you about your attitudes toward education. Could you describe a class or subject you have found interesting?

## Q2 Prompt
Great! Do you prefer studying alone or do you find it more effective to study in groups? Why?

## Q3 Prompt
Interesting. Some people believe that governments should provide free university education for everyone in the country who wants it. Others say it's not something that governments should pay for. What do you think? Why?

## Q4 Prompt
Good points. I just have one more question. Looking to the future, if education becomes more virtual, how do you think that will affect how students interact and learn together?

## Q1 My Polished Response
Honestly, the subject I found most interesting recently is a media studies course. I am actually an undergraduate law student, so I took this as a cross-faculty course to broaden my perspective. The biggest reason I enjoyed it is that it gave me the opportunity to explore cinema history and discover new movies. On top of that, it provided a great space to share different scenarios and analyze various cultural ideologies, which was a refreshing change of pace from my usual legal research.

## Q2 My Polished Response
I actually reject that binary, because the key factor is not the environment, but your own level of focus. The way I see it, even if you study in a group, your success still relies entirely on how hard you apply yourself. For example, if you join a study group but get into a deep state of flow and remain intensely focused on your own work, you are essentially studying alone anyway. Ultimately, true productivity comes down to personal discipline rather than whether other people happen to be in the room with you.

## Q3 My Polished Response
It is simply not practical for a government to spend massive amounts of public money to provide free university education for everyone. The biggest reason is that higher education often works much more efficiently when it is market-driven. When families have to invest their own resources, they are free to carefully evaluate and decide exactly what kind of programs their children should study. Beyond that, relying on a market approach encourages universities to remain competitive and improve their quality.

## Q4 My Polished Response
I tend to think that if education becomes more virtual, it will actually have a positive effect on how students interact. First, human beings have an essential need for face-to-face relationships that screens simply cannot replace. Because virtual classes eliminate commute times, students will actually have much more free time to meet together in person. What is more, staring at a screen all day usually increases a person's desire to socialize offline, meaning they will actively seek out deeper connections after their online classes are over.
