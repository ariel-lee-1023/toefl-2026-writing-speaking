## Q3 My Polished Response
It is simply not practical for a government to spend massive amounts of public money to provide free university education for everyone. The biggest reason is that higher education often works much more efficiently when it is market-driven. When families have to invest their own resources, they are free to carefully evaluate and decide exactly what kind of programs their children should study. Beyond that, relying on a market approach encourages universities to remain competitive and improve their quality.

## Q4 My Polished Response
I tend to think that if education becomes more virtual, it will actually have a positive effect on how students interact. First, human beings have an essential need for face-to-face relationships that screens simply cannot replace. Because virtual classes eliminate commute times, students will actually have much more free time to meet together in person. What is more, staring at a screen all day usually increases a person's desire to socialize offline, meaning they will actively seek out deeper connections after their online classes are over.
