## Title
Campus Recycling Program Feedback

## Prompt
Your university recently introduced a recycling program in all campus buildings. While you support the initiative, you have noticed that many students are confused about which items go in which bins. You would like to share your observations with the sustainability office.
Write an email to Mr. Andersen, the sustainability coordinator. In your email, do the following:
Express your support for the recycling program.
Describe the problems you have observed.
Suggest ways to make the program easier to follow.
Write as much as you can and in complete sentences.

## My Polished Response
Dear Mr. Andersen,

I am writing to express my strong support for the university's new campus-wide recycling initiative. It is a fantastic step toward making our community more sustainable, and I am grateful for your office's hard work in launching it. 

While the program is a great addition to our campus, I have noticed that many students are currently confused about proper waste sorting. In particular, many of my classmates are unsure whether items like coated coffee cups, plastic food containers, or greasy pizza boxes belong in the recycling or trash bins. As a result, items are frequently placed in the wrong containers, which leads to accidental contamination.

To make the system easier to follow, I would like to offer a few quick suggestions. First, placing color-coded signs with visual images above each bin would help students instantly recognize where their waste goes. Second, emailing a brief one-page sorting guide or posting a quick guide in the student newsletters could clarify common misunderstandings. 

Thank you for your time and dedication to improving our campus environment. Please let me know if I can assist in implementing any of these ideas.

Sincerely,  
Ariel

## My Diagnosis
- Limited elaboration on solutions: The original draft only asked the coordinator to send instructions, missing concrete, proactive suggestions to improve the program (e.g., visual signs, newsletters).
- Formatting and mechanical errors: Missing spaces after commas, missing comma after greeting ("Dear Mr.Anderson"), abrupt capitalization ("IF", "SIncerely"), and several typographical errors ("wrting", "calssmates", "availble", "instuction") reduced the text's professional tone and readability.
- Subject-verb agreement and word choice errors: Expressions like "which item go" and "confusing about" degraded language precision.

## My What Changed & Why
- Expanded task fulfillment by introducing concrete examples of student confusion (coffee cups, pizza boxes) and offering specific visual/informational solutions (color-coded signs, newsletter infographics), directly satisfying the highest tier of the ETS writing rubric for elaboration.
- Standardized formal email layout, inserting appropriate punctuation after salutations and closings, while ensuring flawless spelling and mechanics throughout.
- Enhanced sentence variety and syntactic control by combining simple statements into complex structures using subordination and transition signals (e.g., "While the program is...", "To make the system easier to follow...").
