## Title
Minimum Wage Laws

## Prompt
Your professor is teaching a class on economics. Write a post responding to the professor's question.
In your response, you should do the following:
· Express and support your opinion.
· Make a contribution to the discussion in your own words.
An effective response will contain at least 100 words.

Dr. Gupta
Minimum-wage laws are designed to ensure that workers receive fair compensation. Some people argue that raising the minimum wage reduces poverty and improves living standards, while others believe it can lead to job losses and increased costs for businesses. Are minimum-wage laws good or bad for the economy overall?

Claire
Minimum-wage laws help workers afford basic necessities and contribute more to the economy.

Andrew
Increasing the minimum wage can lead to reduced hiring, fewer working hours, and higher costs for businesses.

## My Polished Response
While I understand Andrew's concern that a higher minimum wage might increase operational costs for businesses, I agree with Claire that these laws ultimately strengthen the overall economy by boosting consumer spending. When workers earn more, they do not simply hoard the extra income; they spend it on necessities and local services, which directly drives revenue back into the very businesses Andrew worries about. Furthermore, this increased demand often requires companies to hire more staff to keep up, completely offsetting the initial rise in labor costs. Historically, before such regulations existed, workers frequently had little choice but to accept exploitative wages to survive. Today, a guaranteed minimum wage ensures that companies share their profit growth with the workforce, creating a more sustainable economic environment for everyone involved.

## My Draft
I agree with Claire that raising the minimum wage benefits the economy as a whole because it encourages workers to work harder. Furthermore, raising the minimum wage can stimulate consumption, which is the best engine for contemporary economic growth, and increased consumption leads to better overall profitability for market entities.
Andrew's view that raising the minimum wage compared to current standards might cause companies to hire fewer workers due to the mathematically higher labor costs. On the contrary, I believe most companies can hire more people to handle growing business demands thanks to steady profit growth—this has always been the case. In the past, many workers had to join companies reluctantly under excessively low wages; now, the existence of a minimum wage forces companies to accept the regulation while still hiring enough workers.

## My Key Obstacles Holding You Back from a 5/5
- Sentence Fragment: "Andrew's view that raising the minimum wage compared to current standards might cause companies to hire fewer workers due to the mathematically higher labor costs." (This acts as a noun phrase missing a main verb).
- Absolute Claims: Phrasing like "this has always been the case" lacks the nuanced qualification and hedging expected in the top C1/C2 capability bands.

## My What Changed & Why
- Grammatical Structure: Fixed the fragment by turning the acknowledgment of Andrew's point into a dependent concession clause ("While I understand Andrew's concern that..."). This ensures grammatical completeness while also demonstrating diplomatic disagreement, a key C1 skill.
- Argumentative Cohesion: Connected the logic directly from Claire's point about workers affording basics to your point about company profits. Explaining that workers spend their wages on local services creates a much tighter, more persuasive chain of elaboration.
- C2 Hedging: Replaced the rigid "this has always been the case" with qualifiers like "often requires" and "frequently had little choice." ETS rewards writers who can qualify their opinions in relation to degrees of certainty.
