## Title
Universal Basic Income Debate

## Prompt
Dr. Diaz: We've briefly discussed the concept of a universal basic income (UBI) in our class as a solution to economic inequality. UBI refers to a program that provides all adults with regular cash payments to help cover basic living expenses. Some argue that UBI can provide financial security and reduce poverty, while others believe it can discourage people from working and can negatively impact the economy. Do you think implementing UBI is a good idea? Why or why not?
Claire: I think UBI is a good idea because it can provide a safety net for people who are struggling financially.
Andrew: I don't think UBI is a good idea because it might discourage people from working.

## My Polished Response
While I acknowledge Claire's point that a universal basic income could provide a safety net and open doors to education for individuals who currently lack resources, I agree with Andrew that the potential drawbacks are too severe. Specifically, I share his concern about the negative impact on work incentives, but I would also add that UBI would impose immense fiscal pressure on the state. First, when basic needs are met unconditionally, many individuals may choose to reduce their working hours or withdraw from the workforce entirely, which directly diminishes economic output. Moreover, funding such a massive welfare program would require substantial tax increases. This heavy tax burden could easily stifle corporate innovation and slow long-term economic growth, meaning the initial benefits of UBI would ultimately be overshadowed by broader economic consequences.
