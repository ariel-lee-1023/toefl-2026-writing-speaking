## Title
Urbanization Impacts on Community

## Prompt
Dr. Moreno
We've been discussing the purpose of the criminal justice system. Some scholars believe that the primary goal of the justice system should be rehabilitation, helping offenders develop the skills and mindset needed to reintegrate into society. Others argue that punishment and deterrence should be the focus, as stricter penalties discourage criminal behavior. Which approach do you think is more effective? Why?

Lena
I think rehabilitation should be the priority. Many people who commit crimes come from difficult backgrounds and may lack education or job skills. By helping them address these issues, we reduce the chances of them reoffending and give them an opportunity to become productive members of society.

Sam
I believe punishment is a more effective approach. Strong consequences send a clear message that criminal behavior will not be tolerated. If penalties are too lenient, people may feel that there are no real consequences for breaking the law, which could lead to higher crime rates.

## My Polished Response
While I understand Lena's argument that rehabilitation addresses systemic background issues like education and employment gaps, I agree with Sam that deterrence through strict penalties should remain the primary focus of the justice system. A firm consequences-based model creates an immediate, widespread disincentive to break the law, which protects the broader public far more reliably. First, public safety requires a clear boundary where breaking social contracts incurs predictable, costly consequences. When penalties are overly lenient or focused primarily on social support, potential offenders perceive a low risk factor, which undermines the law's deterrent effect. Second, deterrence operates on the entire population simultaneously, whereas rehabilitation programs require massive institutional resources and often show mixed success depending on offender compliance. For example, strict, predictable sentencing guidelines for major financial fraud or violent crimes discourage potential bad actors long before an offense is ever committed. Although Lena is right that long-term social reintegration is valuable for non-violent offenders, Sam is ultimately more convincing because maintaining a strong legal deterrent is essential to preserving public order.
