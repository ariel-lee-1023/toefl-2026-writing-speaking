## Title
Healthcare vs Education for Social Mobility

## Prompt
Your professor is teaching a class on sociology. Write a post responding to the professor’s question.
In your response, you should do the following.

* Express and support your opinion.

* Make a contribution to the discussion in your own words.

An effective response will contain at least 100 words.
Dr. Diaz
 
We’ve been discussing the concept of social mobility. Some experts argue that attaining a higher education is the key to improving social mobility, while others believe that universal access to quality healthcare and social services plays a more crucial role. Which factor do you think is more important for enhancing social mobility? Why?
 
Andrew
I think higher education is more important for enhancing social mobility. A college degree can open doors to better job opportunities and higher earnings, which can significantly improve one’s social and economic status over time.
Kelly
In my opinion, access to quality healthcare and social services is more crucial. Without good health and basic support, individuals may struggle to pursue education and career opportunities. Ensuring that everyone has access to these services can create a more level playing field.

## My Polished Response
I agree with Kelly that universal access to quality healthcare and social services is more important for enhancing social mobility. Without reliable health coverage and basic support, low-income families remain trapped in short-term survival mode; any unexpected medical bill or untreated chronic condition can wipe out savings and force parents to prioritize immediate income over long-term investments such as education. For example, a parent who cannot afford to take unpaid sick days or risk medical debt is far less likely to encourage a child to pursue college, because the household simply cannot absorb the short-term loss of earnings or the uncertainty. Andrew is right that a college degree opens doors to better jobs and higher earnings, yet those educational opportunities remain out of reach for people who lack baseline physical well-being and social stability. In that sense, healthcare and social services do not replace education; they create the conditions that allow education to function as a genuine mobility pathway. When the foundation is missing, the ladder of higher education has nowhere solid to stand.

## My Draft
I agree with Kelly that universal access to quality healthcare and social services is the foundational driver of social mobility. When low-income families are shielded from catastrophic medical expenses and chronic deprivation, they can redirect their limited resources toward long-term personal and professional growth rather than mere daily survival. Furthermore, the persistent anxiety over unforeseen health emergencies often forces economically vulnerable households into risk-averse financial behaviors, drastically curtailing their willingness to make educational investments even before any acute financial crisis occurs. While Andrew makes a valid point that higher education provides valuable credentials and specialized skills that facilitate entry into lucrative professions, such educational opportunities remain practically out of reach for individuals who lack baseline physical well-being and social stability.
