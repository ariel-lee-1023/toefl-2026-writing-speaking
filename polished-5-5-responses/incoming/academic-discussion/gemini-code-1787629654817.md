## Title
Screen Time in Child Development

## Prompt
Dr. Moreno
We've been discussing the effects of screen time on children's development. As digital devices become a central part of daily life, children are spending more time than ever in front of screens. Some child development experts argue that parents should set strict limits on screen time to protect their children's physical and mental health. Others suggest that moderate, supervised screen use can be educational and beneficial. What is your position on this issue?

Mia
I think parents need to set firm limits on screen time. Too much time in front of devices can affect a child's sleep, attention span, and social skills. Kids should be spending more time playing outside, reading, and interacting face-to-face with others rather than staring at a screen all day.

Jordan
I believe screen time can actually be positive for children when it's managed. There are many high-quality educational apps and programs that help kids learn new skills and explore their interests. Instead of banning screens entirely, parents should focus on guiding what their children watch and how long they use devices.

## My Polished Response
While I acknowledge Mia's concern regarding the immediate developmental risks posed by unmanaged devices, I agree with Jordan that prioritizing supervised, educational screen use ultimately strengthens a child's cognitive development by optimizing their digital literacy. The fundamental mechanism at play here is not merely about enforcing physical time limits, but rather about building healthy digital habits through active parental guidance. First, when children are provided with guided access to high-quality educational platforms, they do not simply consume passive media; instead, they develop the critical evaluation skills required to navigate modern digital environments independently. For example, when parents scaffold device usage by watching educational programs together and discussing the content, children learn to process information actively rather than mindlessly staring at screens. Conversely, relying heavily on top-down strict bans acts as a temporary crutch that fails to build long-term self-regulation. Once children inevitably enter unregulated academic settings, this lack of internalized media management introduces severe risks to their attention span.
