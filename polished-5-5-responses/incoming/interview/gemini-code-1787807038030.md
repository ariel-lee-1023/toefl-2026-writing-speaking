## Title
Sleep Habits and Schedules

## Q1 Prompt
Thank you for joining the study. I'd like to ask you about your sleep habits. What kinds of activities do you usually do before going to bed?

## Q2 Prompt
I see. Some people sleep best when they go to bed early and wake up early, while others prefer to stay up late and sleep in. Which works better for you? And why?

## Q3 Prompt
Interesting. In recent years, many people use their phones or other devices just before going to bed. What kind of effect do you think this is having on how well people sleep?

## Q4 Prompt
Good point. For my final question, some people believe that schools and workplaces should start later in the morning so that people can get more sleep. Do you agree with this idea? Please explain.

## Q1 My Polished Response
There are two primary activities I usually do before going to bed to help me wind down. First, I take a warm shower to signal to my brain that it is time to relax and to relieve physical tension in my body. For instance, stepping out of a warm shower always leaves me feeling refreshed and mentally cleared after a busy day. Second, once I lie down in bed, I like to listen to soothing classical music at a low volume. This peaceful background music creates an ideal atmosphere for quiet reflection and light meditation, allowing me to clear away stress and fall asleep with a positive mindset. Therefore, these night routines ensure I get restful sleep and wake up refreshed.

## Q2 My Polished Response
I definitely sleep best when I stay up late and sleep in, primarily because I prefer completing my most demanding work during quiet night hours. I find that reaching a state of total physical and mental exhaustion late at night makes it much easier for me to fall asleep naturally. For example, working late on complex academic tasks without daytime distractions allows me to focus fully and exhaust my cognitive energy. Once I finish my work late at night, I can fall asleep instantly without tossing and turning. Consequently, keeping a late-night schedule aligns perfectly with my natural productivity cycle and sleep needs.

## Q3 My Polished Response
In my opinion, using smartphones and other electronic devices right before bed has a distinctly negative impact on overall sleep quality. First, browsing short video platforms like TikTok makes it remarkably easy for people to lose track of time and delay their bedtime. For instance, many people intend to watch videos for five minutes but end up scrolling for over an hour, disrupting their natural sleep cycle. Second, the intense stimulation and rapid dopamine hits from modern digital entertainment keep the brain in a hyper-alert state, making it difficult to feel sleepy. As a result, screen time actively prevents people from getting the deep, restorative rest they need.

## Q4 My Polished Response
I disagree with the idea that schools and workplaces should mandate later start times, as simply delaying the schedule does not solve sleep deprivation. When external schedules are pushed back, people usually adjust by staying up even later at night, which neutralizes any potential gain in sleep duration. For example, when high schools delay their morning start time by an hour, students frequently use that flexibility to stay online later rather than sleeping more. I believe the most effective approach is encouraging individuals to align their external obligations with their personal circadian rhythms through flexible scheduling. Therefore, maintaining standard start times while offering individual flexibility is far preferable to a blanket schedule shift.
