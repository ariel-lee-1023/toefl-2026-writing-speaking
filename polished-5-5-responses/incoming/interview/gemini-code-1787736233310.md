## Title
Career Choices and Future Work Trends

## Q1 Prompt
Thank you for participating. I'd like to ask you some questions about jobs and careers. First, from what you've noticed about people around you, what kinds of jobs or careers seem popular among young people where you live?

## Q2 Prompt
I see. When choosing a career, would you mainly follow your own interests or would you listen to your family's advice? Why?

## Q3 Prompt
Interesting. In recent years many people change jobs every few years instead of staying at the same company for a long time. Do you think this is mostly a positive change for workers? Why or why not?

## Q4 Prompt
Good points. One last question. Looking to the future, if computers and machines do more of the work in society, how do you think this will change what people look for in a career?

## Q1 My Polished Response
In my experience, civil service positions are currently among the most popular career paths for young adults in my city. While high-paying tech startups certainly attract ambitious graduates, public sector roles offer unmatched job stability and reliable financial security without the constant threat of corporate layoffs. For instance, many of my peers actively pursue positions in municipal administration or public policy because these roles provide structured working hours and predictable schedules, allowing young professionals to maintain a healthy work-life balance and pursue personal interests outside of work.

## Q2 My Polished Response
When making major career decisions, I rely primarily on my own personal interests rather than my family's expectations. I have a clear understanding of my unique strengths, technical skills, and underlying passions, which ultimately makes me the best judge of my professional trajectory. Furthermore, taking full ownership of my career choices ensures that I stay genuinely motivated, accountable, and deeply engaged in my everyday work, whereas following someone else's plan often leads to frustration and burnout over time.

## Q3 My Polished Response
I do not view changing jobs every few years as a positive trend for most workers. While hopping between companies might occasionally offer quick salary bumps, frequent turnover prevents professionals from building deep trust and meaningful relationships with their colleagues. Moreover, staying with a supportive organization over the long term allows employees to master complex skills and take on higher-level responsibilities, whereas moving constantly can create an unstable environment where workers are always starting from scratch.

## Q4 My Polished Response
As automation and artificial intelligence take over routine technical tasks, I believe people will increasingly seek out careers that prioritize human creativity, strategic thinking, and emotional intelligence. Workers will naturally gravitate toward roles that machines cannot easily replicate, such as executive leadership, complex conflict resolution, and personal counseling. In addition, professionals will place a higher value on positions that offer continuous learning, enabling them to adapt alongside evolving technologies rather than being displaced by them.
