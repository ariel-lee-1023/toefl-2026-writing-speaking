## Title
Outdoor Activities

## Q1 Prompt
Scenario: You have volunteered for a research study about outdoor activities. You will have a short online interview with a researcher. The researcher will ask you some questions.

Thank you for your participation. I have some questions about your outdoor activities. First, what kind of outdoor sports do you or your friends generally like to do? For example, do you like hiking, cycling, tennis, or other types of sport? Thank you.

## Q2 Prompt
When you participate in your favorite outdoor activity, do you prefer to do it alone, or do you like to do it together with your family or friends? Why?

## Q3 Prompt
Interesting. Next, I'd like to get your opinion. Where I live, outdoor activities are becoming increasingly popular. Do you think that in the place you live, the popularity of outdoor activities will increase in the future? Why or why not?

## Q4 Prompt
Good points. I just have one more question. Some people believe that outdoor activities are essential for children's physical and mental health. Do you agree with this idea, or do you think indoor activities can be just as beneficial for children. Explain why you think so.

## Q1 My Polished Response
My friends and I mostly enjoy playing soccer. It's an easy way to get moving after a week of sitting at our desks, and we only need a ball and somewhere to play. We usually meet on weekends and divide into small teams, so everyone gets involved. What I enjoy most is having to react quickly and work with other people. A good pass can be just as satisfying as scoring a goal. Some of my friends also go running, but soccer is usually our first choice when we can get a group together.

## Q2 My Polished Response
I prefer playing soccer with friends because the teamwork is a big part of the fun. Practicing alone can help me improve my ball control, but it doesn't give me the same feeling of working toward a shared goal. When a teammate makes a mistake, we encourage them and try a different approach. That keeps the game enjoyable, even if we're losing. Having a regular group also makes me more likely to show up when I'm tired. Afterward, we often get something to eat, so the activity gives us time to catch up as well.

## Q3 My Polished Response
I don't expect outdoor activities to become much more popular where I live. The main problem is that the weather can make regular plans difficult. If a weekend is extremely hot or rainy, it's easier to choose an indoor gym where conditions are predictable. Once people get used to that convenience, they may keep going even when the weather improves. Online entertainment also gives them plenty of reasons to stay home. Outdoor sports will still have their fans, including me, but I think reliable indoor options will attract more of people's free time.

## Q4 My Polished Response
I agree that outdoor activities should be a regular part of children's lives. They give children space to run, explore, and play with others, which supports both physical activity and social confidence. In a simple game of soccer, for example, children have to agree on rules, take turns, and deal with losing. Those are useful experiences beyond the exercise itself. Indoor activities like drawing or dancing can also be valuable, especially in bad weather. Still, I wouldn't want them to replace outdoor play completely, because children also need chances to move freely and explore their surroundings.
