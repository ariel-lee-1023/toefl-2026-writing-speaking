## Title
Communication Preferences and Relationships

## Q1 Prompt
Thanks for taking part. I'd like to ask you about your communication preferences. How do you usually keep in touch with friends and family who live far away? Through phone calls, text messages, video chats, or another way?

## Q2 Prompt
Great! Can you recall a time when an important conversation changed your mind about something or helped you make a decision? Please explain.

## Q3 Prompt
Interesting. Some people believe listening well is the most important part of good communication, while others believe speaking clearly matters more. What do you think is more important, and why?

## Q4 Prompt
Good points. I just have one last question. In your view, how important is it for friends and family to meet in person, rather than only staying in touch through phones or computers? Why?

## Q1 My Polished Response
To stay connected with family and friends who live far away, I rely on a combination of daily text messages and scheduled weekly video chats. For quick daily updates, instant messaging allows us to share short thoughts, photos, and funny moments without interrupting each other's busy work routines. For instance, sending a quick photo of my lunch keeps us feeling involved in each other's daily lives. On top of that, setting aside time for regular video calls gives us a face-to-face experience where we can catch up on deeper personal news and share meaningful visual moments. Consequently, combining brief daily texts with intimate video calls maintains our strong emotional connection across long distances. Therefore, this dual approach keeps our relationships vibrant and close.

## Q2 My Polished Response
A major turning point in my academic life occurred during my sophomore year, when I received disappointing midterm marks and sat down to have an honest conversation with my older brother. Rather than just offering generic encouragement, he shared his own past struggles with exam anxiety and offered practical advice on changing my daily study habits. For example, he showed me how to break complex math problems into manageable steps and set up a structured review schedule. Following his guidance completely shifted my mindset, boosting my confidence and leading to a significant improvement in my final calculus grade. Ultimately, that single conversation transformed my approach to academic challenges and taught me the value of seeking mentorship.

## Q3 My Polished Response
Although expressing your ideas clearly is undeniably important, I firmly believe that listening well is the single most critical element of effective communication. Active listening allows you to truly grasp the other person's perspective, emotional state, and underlying needs rather than simply waiting for your turn to speak. For instance, when a colleague is stressed about a project, giving them your undivided attention helps you understand their true concerns before offering a solution. Furthermore, attentively digesting someone's words creates a comfortable atmosphere that encourages mutual trust and keeps the conversation flowing productively. Thus, mastering active listening forms the true foundation of meaningful human interaction.

## Q4 My Polished Response
In my view, meeting in person is extraordinarily important for maintaining deep and meaningful relationships over time. Face-to-face interactions allow us to read subtle body language and eye contact, which fosters a genuine emotional connection in ways that digital screens simply cannot replicate. For example, gathering around a dinner table with family allows everyone to pick up on emotional cues and share spontaneous laughter that feels impossible over a video call. Additionally, spending time together in person eliminates digital distractions, allowing us to be fully present with one another. Consequently, making the effort to meet in person builds a far stronger, more enduring bond than relying solely on virtual communication.
