## Title
Exercise Habits

## Q1 Prompt
Scenario: As part of a university research project, you have agreed to take part in a short interview about exercise habits. A graduate student conducting the research will ask you some questions.

Thank you for taking the time to speak with me. I'd like to ask you some questions about your exercise habits. What kind of exercise do you or your friends like? For example, do you like running, swimming, yoga, or other types?

## Q2 Prompt
I see. When you exercise, do you prefer to do it alone, or do you like to exercise with other people? Why?

## Q3 Prompt
Interesting. Next, I'd like to get your opinion. In the past, most people exercised outdoors, but now many people prefer indoor gyms and fitness centers. Do you think that in the future, indoor gyms will become even more popular? Why or why not?

## Q4 Prompt
Good points. I just have one more question. Some people believe that exercising regularly is essential for developing self discipline. Do you agree with this idea? Or do you think there are other activities that can also help build discipline? Explain your answer.

## Q1 My Polished Response
Soccer is my favorite kind of exercise, and several of my friends enjoy it too. I like that I'm concentrating on the game instead of counting how many minutes I've been exercising. There's always something to do, whether it's finding space, passing the ball, or helping a teammate. We usually play a casual match on weekends, so the social side is just as important as the workout. A few friends prefer running because they can fit it around their own schedules. For me, though, having a game to look forward to makes staying active much more enjoyable.

## Q2 My Polished Response
I prefer exercising with other people, mainly because it helps me keep the habit. If I've arranged to meet a friend, I'm less likely to cancel just because I feel tired. The company also makes the activity more enjoyable. During a casual soccer game, we encourage each other and laugh about our mistakes, so it doesn't feel like another task on my list. I still like walking alone when I need some quiet time, but for a regular exercise routine, sharing the effort gives me more motivation than relying entirely on myself.

## Q3 My Polished Response
I think indoor gyms will become more popular because they make exercise easier to fit into a busy life. People can go after work without worrying as much about rain, heat, or whether it's already dark. A gym can also offer several activities in one place, so someone can switch between a treadmill and a class without traveling elsewhere. That convenience matters when time is limited. Membership prices could put some people off, so affordable options will be important. Still, I expect many people to choose the predictability of a gym while keeping outdoor exercise for suitable days.

## Q4 My Polished Response
I agree, especially when self-discipline includes taking care of yourself. You can develop discipline through studying or working, but that doesn't automatically mean you'll protect your health. A student might meet every deadline yet keep putting off exercise because there's always another assignment. Committing to a regular walk means setting aside time for yourself and sticking to it, even when you're busy. It can also mean stopping work when you planned to, instead of letting it fill the whole day. That's why I see exercise as an essential part of a healthy routine, even though other activities also build discipline.
