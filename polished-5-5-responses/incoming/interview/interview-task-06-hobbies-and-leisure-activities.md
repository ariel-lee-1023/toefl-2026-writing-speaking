## Title
Hobbies and Leisure Activities

## Q1 Prompt
Scenario: You have agreed to participate in a university research study about hobbies and leisure activities. You will have a short online interview with a researcher. The researcher will ask you some questions.

Thank you for taking the time to speak with me about your hobbies and leisure activities. First, when you engage in your favorite hobby, do you prefer to do it alone, or do you like to do it with your family or friends? Why?

## Q2 Prompt
Thank you. And do you think it is better to practice hobbies indoors, like playing chess, or outdoors, like playing frisbee? Why?

## Q3 Prompt
Interesting. Next, I'd like to get your opinion. Many people have the same hobbies as their friends. Do you think it is necessary for good friends to share hobbies? Why or why not?

## Q4 Prompt
Good points. I just have one more question. Some people believe that having hobbies is essential for being happy. Do you agree with this idea? Or do you think you can live a happy life without having hobbies? Explain your answer.

## Q1 My Polished Response
For my favorite hobby, playing soccer, I definitely prefer being with friends. The enjoyment comes from reacting to each other and working as a team, so practicing alone would only give me part of the experience. When we play, someone might suggest a different position or show me a better way to pass. I learn something while spending time with people I like. We often hang around afterward to chat, which is especially valuable when everyone has a busy week. I enjoy some quiet activities too, but soccer gives me a reason to make time for my friends.

## Q2 My Polished Response
I'd usually choose an outdoor hobby because I spend so much of my day sitting inside. Going to a park gives me a change of setting and makes it easier to leave my phone alone for a while. With soccer, for example, I have to pay attention to the ball and the people around me, so I stop thinking about messages or unfinished work. Indoor hobbies like chess can be enjoyable, especially when the weather is bad. But when conditions are good, I prefer an activity that gets me moving and fully involved in what's happening around me.

## Q3 My Polished Response
I don't think good friends need to share the same hobbies. A common interest can make it easier to start a conversation, but a lasting friendship depends more on listening, trust, and making time for each other. For example, one friend might love photography while another prefers soccer. They can still enjoy meeting for dinner and talking about their experiences, even if they never join the same activity. Different interests can actually give them more to talk about. I'd rather have a friend who takes my concerns seriously than someone who simply enjoys all the same things.

## Q4 My Polished Response
Hobbies can make life happier, but I don't think everyone needs a specific hobby to be happy. People can also find satisfaction in close relationships, meaningful work, or caring for their families. Someone who spends an evening cooking with their children might feel deeply content without thinking of cooking as a hobby. What matters is having experiences that feel worthwhile, rather than constantly chasing achievements or comparing yourself with others. Personally, soccer gives me that kind of enjoyment and connection. But another person could find it in everyday family life, without adding a separate activity to their schedule.
