## Title
Travel Habits

## Q1 Prompt
Scenario: You have volunteered for a research study at your university about travel habits. You will have a short online interview with a researcher. The researcher will ask you some questions.

Thank you for speaking with me about your travel habits. First, have your family or friends ever traveled to see a sporting event? For example, have they traveled to see a football game, basketball game, or other types of sports? Why or why not?

## Q2 Prompt
I see. When you travel, do you prefer to plan the details of your trip in advance, or do you prefer to be spontaneous and explore your destination without sticking to a plan? Why?

## Q3 Prompt
Interesting. Next, I'd like to get your opinion. In recent years, there has been a trend towards eco-friendly travel, where people try to minimize their environmental impact while traveling, like using public transportation instead of taking a taxi. Do you think that in the future, more people will adopt eco-friendly travel habits? Why or why not?

## Q4 Prompt
Good points. I just have one more question. Some people believe that traveling is essential for building confidence and independence. Do you agree with this idea? Or do you think there are other ways to develop these qualities? Explain why you think so.

## Q1 My Polished Response
Yes, a couple of my friends once traveled to another city to watch a soccer match. They usually watched their favorite team on television, but they wanted to experience a live game at least once. They took a train, stayed for one night, and made the match the main event of the trip. What they talked about afterward was the atmosphere, especially hearing thousands of fans cheer together. I generally prefer watching at home because I can see the action clearly, but I understand why sharing that experience made the journey worthwhile for them.

## Q2 My Polished Response
I prefer to plan the important parts of a trip in advance. I usually book my transport and accommodation first, then choose one or two things I really want to do each day. That way, I don't waste time worrying about where to stay or how to get somewhere. For example, if a museum requires a reservation, I can arrange that before leaving home. However, I leave some gaps for a walk or a restaurant I discover along the way. Having a basic plan actually makes it easier for me to relax and enjoy unexpected opportunities.

## Q3 My Polished Response
I think more people will adopt environmentally friendly travel habits, especially when those choices are convenient and affordable. Concern for the environment helps, but people also care about their budgets and schedules. If a train takes visitors directly from the airport to the city center, many will choose it over an expensive taxi. Clear route information would make that decision even easier for someone visiting for the first time. However, a bus that runs only twice a day is a harder choice. So I expect the biggest changes where greener options also solve practical problems.

## Q4 My Polished Response
Travel can build confidence and independence, but I don't think it's essential. What matters is having responsibility and solving problems without someone doing everything for you. On a trip, that might mean finding your hotel after getting off at the wrong station. But a student can develop the same skills by managing a budget, cooking meals, or organizing a university event. For example, arranging a small event involves making decisions and dealing with unexpected changes. Travel creates opportunities to practice these skills, but people who can't afford to travel still have plenty of ways to grow.
