## Q1 My Polished Response
From what I have observed among my peers, civil service positions are currently the most popular career choice for young people. First, public sector jobs offer exceptional job stability and financial security without the intense pressure of corporate competition. Second, these roles typically provide manageable working hours and a predictable schedule, allowing young professionals to maintain a healthy work-life balance and pursue personal hobbies.

## Q2 My Polished Response
When making career choices, I rely entirely on my own personal interests rather than my family's advice. First, I have a clear understanding of my own strengths, skills, and passions, which makes me the best judge of my career path. Second, taking full ownership of my professional choices ensures that I remain genuinely motivated, accountable, and engaged in my work every day.

## Q3 My Polished Response
I do not view changing jobs every few years as a positive trend for workers. First, switching companies frequently prevents colleagues from building strong, stable working relationships and deep trust within a team. Second, constant turnover creates an unstable work environment, which often hinders long-term career growth and skill development compared to staying at a supportive company.

## Q4 My Polished Response
In the future, as automation and computers take over routine work, I believe people will look for careers that prioritize human creativity, strategic thinking, and emotional intelligence. First, workers will seek roles that machines cannot easily replicate, such as leadership, complex problem-solving, and personal counseling. Second, people will place a higher value on positions that offer continuous learning and adaptability, allowing them to collaborate effectively alongside artificial intelligence.

## My Diagnosis
- Argumentative contradictions and lack of logical coherence: Q3 contained conflicting claims, stating that job-hopping shows firms are "less flexible" while simultaneously asserting that workers "cannot change their current firm".
- Lexical inaccuracies and non-standard vocabulary: Misused specialized terms, including using "instructions" instead of "skills/strengths", "motor abilities" instead of "technical abilities", and applying "opt-out" out of context.
- Grammatical agreement and clause construction errors: Uncountable noun agreement errors ("very few actual work") and improper verb complementation ("don't let colleagues to have") restricted precise meaning.

## My What Changed & Why
- Standardized Structural Scaffolding (C-D-E-F): Replaced disjointed claims with an explicit opening thesis followed by two logically aligned supporting points introduced by "First," and "Second,".
- Precise Lexical Replacements: Upgraded imprecise or misused vocabulary to standard academic English ("personal strengths and skills" instead of "instructions", "manageable working hours" instead of "few actual work", and "human creativity and strategic thinking" instead of "motor abilities").
- Logical Alignment & Coherence: Resolved the argument contradiction in Q3 by framing the issue consistently around the loss of team stability and hindered long-term career growth.
