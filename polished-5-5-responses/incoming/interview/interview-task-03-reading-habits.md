## Title
Reading Habits

## Q1 Prompt
Scenario: You have agreed to participate in a research study about reading habits. You will have a short online interview with a researcher. The researcher will ask you some questions.

Thank you for taking the time to speak with me. I'd like to ask you some questions about your reading habits. First, what kind of books do your family or friends generally like to read? For example, do they prefer fiction, non-fiction, biographies, or other types?

## Q2 Prompt
I see. When you read your favorite book, do you prefer to read it in a quiet place, or do you like to read it where there is some background noise? Why?

## Q3 Prompt
Interesting. Next, I'd like to get your opinion. If you have time to read one novel, do you think it more worthwhile to read an old classic that many readers love, or would you choose a newly published novel that tells a modern story? Why?

## Q4 Prompt
Good points. I just have one more question. Some people believe that reading helps improve one's imagination and creativity. Do you agree with this idea, or do you think there are other activities that can also enhance these skills? Explain why you think so.

## Q1 My Polished Response
Many of my family members and friends enjoy historical fiction. They like stories that bring an unfamiliar period to life while giving them characters they can care about. For example, a novel about an ordinary family during a major historical change can make that period feel much more personal than a list of dates. Afterward, they often talk about the choices the characters made and whether they would have acted differently. The story can also make them curious enough to read about the actual history. That mixture of storytelling and discovery is what appeals to them.

## Q2 My Polished Response
I prefer a little background music when I read, especially quiet classical piano. Complete silence sometimes makes my mind wander, while gentle music helps me settle into the book. I might play something by Chopin or Bach at a low volume and put my phone out of reach. Music without lyrics works best because I don't get caught up in someone else's words. I wouldn't choose a busy cafe, though, since conversations would pull my attention away from the story. For me, soft music creates a calm atmosphere without making the room feel completely silent.

## Q3 My Polished Response
If I had time for just one novel, I'd choose an old classic. Reading is partly a way for me to step away from everyday pressures, so a story from another period offers a welcome change. At the same time, questions about love, ambition, or family can still feel familiar. For example, I might disagree with a character's decision but understand the fear behind it. That makes the book interesting beyond its historical setting. A new novel could be excellent too, but I'm more drawn to a story that lets me explore a different world.

## Q4 My Polished Response
I agree that reading can develop imagination and creativity, especially when people actively think about the story. A book gives you words, but you have to picture the setting and imagine how the characters feel. Discussing it afterward can take that process further. For example, a friend might see a character as brave while I see them as careless. Explaining our views could lead us to imagine a different ending. Drawing or making films can build creativity too, so reading isn't the only route. Its value is that it gives us ideas we can question and reshape.
