## Title
Education and Study Habits

## Q1 Prompt
Thank you for joining the study. I'd like to ask you about your attitudes toward education. Could you describe a class or subject you have found interesting?

## Q2 Prompt
Great! Do you prefer studying alone or do you find it more effective to study in groups? Why?

## Q3 Prompt
Interesting. Some people believe that governments should provide free university education for everyone in the country who wants it. Others say it's not something that governments should pay for. What do you think? Why?

## Q4 Prompt
Good points. I just have one more question. Looking to the future, if education becomes more virtual, how do you think that will affect how students interact and learn together?

## Q1 My Polished Response
The class I found most interesting recently was a cross-faculty media studies course on film history and culture. As an undergraduate law student, taking this course provided a refreshing change of pace from my heavy legal coursework and case analysis. The course gave me a fascinating opportunity to explore cinema history and analyze how cultural identities and political ideologies are reflected on screen. Engaging in discussions about visual media broadened my academic perspective and deepened my critical thinking skills.

## Q2 My Polished Response
I strongly prefer studying alone because personal solitude allows me to achieve a deep state of concentration. While collaborative group study can certainly be useful for brainstorming, working independently eliminates social distractions and allows me to set my own pacing. For example, when preparing for complex exams, sitting quietly in a library study pod helps me enter a flow state where I can master intricate concepts far more efficiently. Ultimately, independent study gives me complete control over my learning environment and productivity.

## Q3 My Polished Response
I do not think it is practical or efficient for governments to provide fully free university education for everyone. While public subsidies can improve accessibility, higher education generally operates much more effectively when it is market-driven. When students and families invest their own financial resources, they tend to evaluate degree programs more carefully and remain deeply committed to their studies. Furthermore, a market-based model encourages universities to compete for talent by continuously upgrading their facilities and teaching quality.

## Q4 My Polished Response
Looking to the future, I believe that if education becomes more virtual, it will actually have positive effects on how students interact and learn together. While online platforms handle routine lectures efficiently, human beings have a fundamental need for face-to-face connection that digital screens simply cannot replace. Eliminating daily commute times gives students more flexibility to organize meaningful, in-person study sessions and social gatherings outside of class. Consequently, virtual learning will encourage students to seek out deeper, more intentional offline relationships.
