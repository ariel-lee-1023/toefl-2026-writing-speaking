## Q3 My Polished Response
I believe schools should actively integrate digital devices into young children’s learning environments. First, interactive technology makes acquiring new information far more efficient and engaging for young minds. Second, early exposure to phones and tablets builds essential digital literacy, which children need to navigate our modern, tech-driven world successfully. Therefore, while outdoor play is valuable, incorporating technology into education is crucial.

## Q4 My Polished Response
In my view, new technology generally makes life better, even though it can sometimes amplify underlying human problems. On the positive side, modern tools allow us to access information and solve complex tasks far more efficiently, which enhances overall convenience and daily well-being. However, technology cannot fix existing personal issues, such as poor communication in personal relationships, and it often makes those existing friction points more obvious. Overall, as long as we address our personal habits, technology brings far more benefits than harms.

## My Diagnosis
- Inconsistent and hesitant stances on preference prompts: Prompt 3 split focus between outdoor play and electronic devices before choosing a stance, violating the direct Commitment rule (C in C-D-E-F).
- Non-standard phrasing and imprecise word choices: Used awkward constructions such as "stranger things I use to use", "align the knowledge", "any host AI don't get bored", and "discuss about learning tasks".
- Sentence fragmentation and trailing prepositional clauses: Sentences in Q1 and Q3 ran on with complex, ungrammatical relative clauses ("medium that I access information such as my daily schedule and others requests from me").

- Standardized Response Architecture (C-D-E-F): Forced an explicit, one-sentence thesis statement at the opening of every answer to eliminate hesitation and satisfy ETS criteria for directness.
- Natural Collocations & Vocabulary Refinement: Replaced awkward phrases with precise academic and natural expressions ("unfamiliar topic" instead of "stranger things", "digital literacy" instead of "electronic literacy", and "ask follow-up questions" instead of "consistently to question").
- Grammatical Cleanup: Corrected preposition overuse ("discuss learning tasks" instead of "discuss about"), fixed agreement errors ("AI will never grow tired" instead of "any host AI don't get bored"), and shortened sentences to ensure clear syntactic connections.
