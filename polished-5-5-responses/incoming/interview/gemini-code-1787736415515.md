## Title
Communication Preferences and Personal Decisions

## Q1 Prompt

## Q2 Prompt

## Q3 Prompt
Interesting. Some people believe listening well is the most important part of good communication, while others believe speaking clearly matters more. What do you think is more important, and why?

## Q4 Prompt
Good points. I just have one last question. In your view, how important is it for friends and family to meet in person, rather than only staying in touch through phones or computers? Why?

## Q1 My Polished Response
While traditional phone calls certainly have their place, I usually rely on a combination of daily text messages and scheduled weekly video chats to stay connected with family and friends who live far away. For daily updates, instant messaging allows us to share quick thoughts, photos, and life moments without interrupting each other's busy work routines. On top of that, setting aside time for regular video calls gives us a face-to-face experience where we can catch up on deeper personal news and read each other's facial expressions almost as if we were meeting in person.

## Q2 My Polished Response
A major turning point in my academic life occurred during my sophomore year when I received disappointing marks on my midterms and sat down for a candid conversation with my older brother. Rather than just offering vague encouragement, he shared his own past struggles with exam anxiety and gave me practical advice on changing my study habits from passive re-reading to active recall. Hearing his perspective made me realize that my previous methods were inefficient, which prompted me to completely revamp my routine and ultimately rebuild my academic confidence.

## Q3 My Polished Response
Although speaking clearly is undeniably important for expressing ideas, I believe that listening well is the single most critical element of effective communication. Active listening allows you to truly grasp another person's perspective, emotional state, and underlying needs rather than simply waiting for your turn to talk. Furthermore, when you show someone that you are attentively digesting their words, it creates a comfortable atmosphere that encourages mutual trust and keeps conversations flowing constructively, even during heated disagreements.

## Q4 My Polished Response
In my view, meeting in person is extraordinarily important for sustaining deep, meaningful relationships over time. Face-to-face interactions allow us to read subtle body language and eye contact, which fosters genuine emotional resonance in ways that digital screens simply cannot replicate. Additionally, gathering in person eliminates the constant notifications and digital distractions typical of phone calls, allowing us to be fully present with one another and build lasting memories.
