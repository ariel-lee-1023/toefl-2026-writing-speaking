## Title

## Q1 Prompt
Thanks for taking part. I'd like to ask you about cities and neighborhoods. What kinds of places do people often visit in your local area? For example, do they usually go to parks, shops, libraries, or somewhere else?

## Q2 Prompt
I see. If you could design your own neighborhood, what kinds of places would you want to have nearby? And why is that?

## Q3 Prompt
Interesting. These days, many people in cities choose to walk or bike instead of driving cars. What do you think are the advantages or disadvantages of this change?

## Q4 Prompt
Good points. I just have one last question. In the future, do you think more people will choose to live in big cities, or in smaller, less busy places? Please explain.

## Q3 My Polished Response
I believe the shift toward walking and biking in urban areas offers far more advantages than disadvantages. First, choosing to walk or cycle significantly reduces carbon emissions and air pollution in cities, creating a cleaner environment. Second, daily active transportation improves residents' physical health and overall quality of life through regular, accessible exercise.

## Q4 My Polished Response

First, smaller cities foster closer, more stable personal relationships, whereas fast-paced big cities can feel isolating and overwhelming. Second, smaller urban areas offer a lower cost of living and less overcrowding, allowing residents to enjoy a wider variety of outdoor recreational activities.

## My Diagnosis
- Misinterpreting task type in Q1: Answered with normative advice ("people should visit...") instead of describing actual local behavior as requested by the prompt.
- Non-standard phrasing and awkward expressions: Used imprecise phrases such as "make our life longer", "reduces carbon consumption", and "allow them to do some more free activities".
- Lack of explicit structural markers and varied vocabulary: Relied on repetitive cause-and-effect connectors ("because... and also...") rather than clear signposts ("First," "Second,").
