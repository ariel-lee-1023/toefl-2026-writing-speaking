## Title
Animal Shelter Tour

## Prompt
Scenario: a supervisor training you on leading public tours at an animal shelter

1. Begin with a greeting at the reception desk.
2. Then walk visitors past our dog kennels.
3. Take them to the cat lounge to meet the cats.
4. Then visit the small pet area down the hallway.
5. Stay with visitors in the meet-and-greet room while they decide.
6. You may also show visitors the outdoor walking yard behind the dog area.
7. Follow the hand-washing rules and remember that some animals can become nervous quickly.

## My Chunking & Memory Strategy
Boundaries fall naturally along functional syntactic breaks: bare imperatives open initial action chunks, followed immediately by prepositional place or purpose phrases. In sentence 5, the subordinating conjunction 'while' signals a temporal time chunk ('while they decide'), isolating the primary imperative action ('stay with visitors in the meet-and-greet room'). In sentence 6, the modal cue phrase 'you may also' establishes the softened command frame, allowing the multi-modifier location description to be retained as a single cohesive place chunk ('the outdoor walking yard behind the dog area'). In sentence 7, the coordinating conjunction 'and' and complementizer 'that' break a 13-word rule statement into three manageable units: action 1 ('follow the hand-washing rules'), action 2 ('and remember'), and condition ('that some animals can become nervous quickly'). Recognizing these structural cue words prevents memory overload on 12–13 word sentences.

## My Pronunciation Focus
- Compressed function words: 'with' and 'at' in place/manner chunks; 'past' and 'down' in place chunks; 'to' in purpose infinitives; 'while' in time clauses; and modal 'can' in condition clauses.
- Word endings (-s / -ed / final t-d): exact delivery required on plural nouns 'visitors', 'kennels', 'cats', 'rules', and 'animals', while ensuring singular nouns like 'yard' do not receive an invented plural ending.
- Rhythm & stress: stress primary beats on core content words ('BE-gin', 'GREE-ting', 'KEN-nels', 'CAT LOUNGE', 'VIS-it', 'MEET-and-GREET', 'HAND-wash-ing'); maintain smooth continuity through compound hyphenated descriptors without inserting artificial pauses.
- Content words to say crisply: initial discourse marker 'then' (/ðɛn/) to prevent misperception as 'don't'; 'visit' (/vɪzɪt/) with firm voiced labiodental /v/ to prevent confusion with 'view'; 'pet' (/pɛt/ with short /ɛ/) to avoid slipping into 'pad'; and compound linking in 'meet-and-greet' (/miːtn̩ɡriːt/) to avoid rhythm substitution.
