## Title
Home Wi-Fi Router Setup

## Prompt
Scenario: setting up a home Wi-Fi router

1. Adjust the antenna on the device for a stronger signal.
2. Begin by plugging in the power cord.
3. Attach the internet cable as shown.
4. Check the indicator lights to ensure the router is working.
5. Log in to the system using your computer's keyboard.
6. You can then create a new safe and secure Wi-Fi password for your network.
7. If you experience issues with the connection, try restarting your computer.

## My Chunking & Memory Strategy
Early sentences are pure action-plus-place/purpose or action-plus-manner and can be held as one or two chunks. Medium sentences each add one quiet function-word chunk (*to ensure the router is working, using your computer's keyboard*). The two long sentences decide the set: sentence 6 is a softened command whose *for your network* is a free purpose boundary; sentence 7 is a classic *if\ldots then* whose comma after *connection* is a free boundary. Reconstruct any missing function word from the chunk type. Never rebuild the sentence from your own grammar; the chunks exist only to keep the speaker’s exact wording long enough to finish.

## My Pronunciation Focus
- Compressed function words: the / on / for / a / by / in / as / to / using / your / you / can / then / and / if / with — each recoverable from its chunk type (place → on/in/to, purpose → for/to, manner → as/using, condition → if).
- Word endings (-s / -ed / final t-d): antenna, device, signal, plugging, cord, cable, indicator, lights, router, working, system, computer's, keyboard, password, network, issues, connection, restarting, computer. Content words that carry no ending (adjust, begin, attach, check, ensure, log, create, experience, try) must stay clean.
- Rhythm & stress: imitate the speaker’s stress on the content words and the slight pause after the *if* clause or after *You can then*; do not substitute your own conversational rhythm.
- Content words to say crisply: antenna, indicator, router, computer's, Wi-Fi, password, experience, restarting — any blur produces the ambiguity the rubric penalizes.
