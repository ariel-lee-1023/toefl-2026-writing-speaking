## My Chunking & Memory Strategy
In item 2, keep "We have maps" as the main proposition and "to help you find your way" as its purpose; find belongs inside the help phrase, so it does not need a separate memory slot. Item 3 packages "It's a good idea" as a suggestion frame, followed by the action and destination. In item 5, "Don't forget" is a negative reminder frame: its negative applies to forgetting, not to budgeting time. Item 6 begins with a time frame, but the main structure is an opportunity statement, "is your last chance", followed by "to see the special exhibit" and the topic phrase "on photography". Item 7 has a relative clause introduced by "many of which"; which refers back to events, and the whole clause occupies one chunk. ## My Pronunciation Focus

Preserve for in "for our popular gift shop" and "for future events". In the final clause, the recoverable grammatical frame is "many of which are", not just which.
- Word endings (-s / -ed / final t-d): Maps, items, and events carry plural endings. Counter, lobby, idea, tour, shop, week, chance, and calendar stay singular. These forms must be heard rather than inferred solely from the chunk shape.
