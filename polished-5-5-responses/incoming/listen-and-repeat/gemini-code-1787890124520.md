## Title
Hotel Front Desk Check-In

## Prompt
Scenario: a supervisor training you on hotel front desk management software

1. begin by typing the reservation number.
2. then check the guest details on screen.
3. Choose an available room from the list to confirm.
4. Open the ID scan tab on the system.
5. then enter the payment details before confirming the booking.
6. You can press the encoding button to activate a new room keycard.
7. For the best experience, you will need to add any special requests when the guest checks in.

## My Chunking & Memory Strategy
Boundaries fall naturally along functional syntactic breaks: bare imperatives open short sentences, followed by place or time prepositional phrases. In sentence 6, the modal cue phrase 'you can' establishes the softened command frame, allowing the remaining words to be grouped as an object chunk ('press the encoding button') and an infinitive purpose chunk ('to activate a new room keycard'). In sentence 7, the preposition 'for' opens a front-loaded condition chunk ('for the best experience'), creating a natural auditory boundary before the main modal clause ('you will need to add...') and the temporal clause ('when the guest checks in'). Recognizing these structural cue words allows long 12–16 word sentences to be retained as three manageable cognitive chunks rather than isolated lexical items.

## My Pronunciation Focus
- Compressed function words: 'by' in manner chunks; 'on' and 'from' in place chunks; 'an' before vowel-initial adjectives ('an available'); 'to' in purpose infinitives ('to activate'); and 'for' in condition chunks.
- Word endings (-s / -ed / final t-d): exact delivery required on plural nouns 'details' and 'requests', third-person verb 'checks', while avoiding invented plural endings on singular nouns like 'reservation' or 'booking'.
- Rhythm & stress: stress primary beats on content words ('BE-gin', 'TYP-ing', 'RES-er-VA-tion', 'ROOM', 'I-D SCAN', 'EN-co-ding'); maintain even rhythm without inserting artificial pauses inside cohesive verb-object phrases.
- Content words to say crisply: 'room' (/uːm/ with firm final /m/) to prevent mishearing as 'runes'; clear letter-name articulation for 'ID scan' (/aɪ diː skæn/); and plural 'requests' (/ts/) to maintain full prompt fidelity.
