## Title
Campus Dining Hall

## Prompt
Scenario: You are working at a campus dining hall. A shift supervisor is training you to explain your responsibilities to new student workers. Listen to the supervisor and repeat what the supervisor says. Repeat only once.

1. Put on clean gloves before you handle food.
2. Check that food is at the right temperature.
3. Keep dishes covered at your station during your shift.
4. Watch food levels at your station, and ask for more as needed.
5. Clean your work station often during your shift to keep it tidy.
6. To keep your work space organized, store items on the correct shelf, and grouped by type.
7. If you see a safety issue, report it right away so it can be addressed.

## My Chunking & Memory Strategy
Item 1 joins putting on gloves to a before-clause, so the action and its timing form two memory units. In item 2, that introduces what must be checked; it does not describe the noun food as a relative clause would. Item 4 combines two actions with and; "as needed" qualifies asking for more and must survive at the end. Item 5 adds both a time phrase and a purpose clause, making three units. Item 6 combines a front-loaded purpose with storage information; "and grouped by type" is retained exactly as supplied, despite its uneven coordination. It is not silently changed to "and group them by type". Item 7 places the condition first, then the immediate action, then the reason/result. There is no then in the source, so recognizing an if-conditional does not authorize adding one. The 16-word item 6 exceeds the course's usual 13–15-word range but still fits four chunks. ## My Pronunciation Focus
- Compressed function words: Before marks the timing in "before you handle food"; at marks a station location or a temperature value; during marks the shift. To introduces purpose in "to keep it tidy" and "To keep your work space organized". Preserve both and and by in "and grouped by type", and the full passive sequence "so it can be addressed".
- Word endings (-s / -ed / final t-d): Preserve gloves, dishes, levels, and items as plurals, and covered, needed, organized, grouped, and addressed with their original endings. Put, right, shift, report, and correct end in t, while food ends in d. Handle, Check, Watch, ask, Clean, keep, store, station, shelf, type, and issue have no added plural or past ending in these sentences. Chunk types cannot supply the missing endings.
- Rhythm & stress: No audio was supplied, so the speaker's stress pattern and any rhythm substitution cannot be identified. The following are text-based phrasing suggestions only: Keep the and-linked second action audible in item 4 and retain the final passive clause in item 7. The commas in item 6 suggest boundaries, but the audio must determine their actual duration.
