## Title
Pottery Wheel Basics

## Prompt
Scenario: An instructor guiding a student through the steps of shaping clay on a pottery wheel.

1. We start with a small ball of clay.
2. Press the clay onto the wheel head.
3. Wet your hands with the sponge for better grip.
4. Steady the spinning clay by pressing both palms in.
5. Slow the wheel down before pulling the sides up.
6. Smooth the rim with a sponge and remove any extra clay from the surface.
7. To remove the finished pot, slide a wire under the base and lift carefully.

## My Chunking & Memory Strategy
Boundaries follow natural syntactic breaks (action → place/manner → purpose). For long sentences like #6 and #7, structural anchors break down memory load: the conjunction "and" splits two action chunks in #6, while the front-loaded purpose infinitive ("To remove the finished pot") in #7 provides a natural pause boundary that isolates the subsequent sequential commands.

## My Pronunciation Focus
- Compressed function words: "with a" / "ball of" in object chunks; "onto the" / "with the" in location chunks; "for" / "to" in purpose chunks.
- Word endings (-s / -ed / final t-d): hands, palms, sides, finished. Ensure content nouns like "pot", "rim", and "wheel" carry no added consonants or ending distortions.
- Rhythm & stress: Maintain primary stress on core content words ("wheel head", "steady", "spinning", "rim", "pot").
- Content words to say crisply: wheel (/wiːl/), steady (/ˈstɛdi/), rim (/rɪm/), pot (/pɑt/).
