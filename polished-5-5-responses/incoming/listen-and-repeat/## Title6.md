## Title
Fire Safety Evacuation Drill

## Prompt
Scenario: a workplace fire-safety or evacuation-drill briefing

1. Start by locating the nearest exit to you.
2. Use the extinguisher for small fires.
3. Activate the alarm quickly if you happen to smell smoke.
4. Proceed to the designated area outside.
5. Stay calm and follow the signs for the evacuation route.
6. Assist your colleagues who may need extra help to get safely away from the danger zone.
7. The elevators will not be working during a fire drill, so use the stairs instead.

## My Chunking & Memory Strategy
Short sentences are pure action-plus-place or action-plus-purpose and can be held as one or two chunks. Medium sentences each add one quiet function-word chunk (*if you happen to smell smoke, to the designated area, and follow\ldots for the evacuation route*). The two long sentences decide the set: sentence 6 is a *who*-relative clause plus purpose; the relative clause itself is a free boundary. Sentence 7 is a statement followed by a *so*-command; the comma before *so* is a free boundary. Reconstruct any missing function word from the chunk type. Never rebuild the sentence from your own grammar; the chunks exist only to keep the speaker’s exact wording long enough to finish.

## My Pronunciation Focus
- Compressed function words: by / the / to / for / if / you / and / who / may / from / during / a / so — each recoverable from its chunk type (place → to/from/outside, purpose → for/to, condition → if, serial → and/so).
- Word endings (-s / -ed / final t-d): locating, nearest, exit, extinguisher, fires, alarm, smoke, designated, area, signs, evacuation, route, colleagues, help, danger, zone, elevators, working, drill, stairs. Content words that carry no ending (start, use, activate, proceed, stay, follow, assist, get, use) must stay clean.
- Rhythm & stress: imitate the speaker’s stress on the content words and the slight pause after the *if* clause, after the *who* clause, or before *so*; do not substitute your own conversational rhythm.
- Content words to say crisply: locating, extinguisher, designated, evacuation, colleagues, danger, elevators, drill — any blur produces the ambiguity the rubric penalizes.
