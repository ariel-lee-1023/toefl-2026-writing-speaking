# Business Ethics Versus Profitability

## Prompt
Dr. Gupta: We've been discussing the importance of ethical behavior in business. Ethical practices, like fair wages or sustainable production policies, can enhance a company's reputation and build trust with customers. Some argue that companies should prioritize ethical practices even if it affects profitability, while others believe that profit should always come first. What is your opinion on this issue?

Claire: Ethical practices should be prioritized in business. Building trust with customers and maintaining a positive reputation can lead to long-term success. Even if it affects short-term profitability, ethical behavior ensures long-term, sustainable growth.

Andrew: Profit should come first in business decisions, especially in cases when the business is new and needs to grow. Companies need to be financially healthy to continue operating and providing jobs. Ethical practices are important, but they should not compromise a company's ability to be profitable.

## My Polished Response
I agree with Andrew that businesses must maintain steady profit margins to sustain basic operations, fund research and development, and meet payroll obligations. Consequently, when evaluating a firm's primary objective, commercial viability naturally takes precedence. While Claire correctly observes that ethical practices like fair-wage policies or sustainable sourcing foster valuable customer goodwill, I view these initiatives as strategic investments designed to secure long-term profitability rather than self-justifying goals. Pursuing ethical ideals without regard to margins does not inherently keep a business solvent. Furthermore, if fiscal health is discarded as the core governing constraint, a company risks insolvency—thereby extinguishing its ability to act as an ethical agent altogether. Ultimately, because ethical corporate management functions as a means to achieve market longevity, securing fundamental profitability must remain the primary decision-making principle.

## My Draft
I agree with Andrew’s point that businesses require profits to sustain normal product development and manufacturing operations, distribute wealth to employees, and fuel their own growth. Therefore, when considering a company's ultimate priority, profit naturally takes precedence. While Claire correctly highlights that ethical corporate policies often foster long-term commercial success, I view this as an ethical framework applied within the pursuit of profitability. Seeking ethics for its own sake does not inherently generate profit. Furthermore, if profitability is not held as a core governing principle, the blind pursuit of ethics risks stripping the company of its status as an ethical agent simply because it will cease to exist. Ultimately, because ethical management serves as a means to achieve long-term success—which fundamentally requires survival and profitability—profit must take priority over ethics as a decision-making principle.
