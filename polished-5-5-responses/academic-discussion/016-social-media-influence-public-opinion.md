# Social Media Influence Public Opinion

## Prompt
Your professor is teaching a class on social sciences. Write a post responding to the professor’s question.
In your response, you should do the following.
Express and support your opinion.
Make a contribution to the discussion in your own words.
An effective response will contain at least 100 words.

Dr. Achebe
We’ve been discussing the role of social media in shaping public opinion. Social media platforms have become powerful tools not only for communication and information sharing, but also for influencing public opinion. Do you think social media has too much influence on public opinion? Why or why not?

Kelly
I believe social media has a positive influence on public opinion. It provides a powerful platform for people to raise awareness about important issues that affect peoples’ everyday lives. It can also mobilize people for social causes and create a sense of community.

Paul
In my opinion, social media has a negative influence on public opinion. Because people often frequent sites that discuss issues that they are interested in, social media can lead to people only hearing opinions that reinforce their own beliefs. This can lead to a fragmented society.

## My Polished Response
While I agree with Paul's analysis regarding social media's role in creating a fragmented society, I would locate the underlying issue more precisely: digital platforms systematically incentivize conflict over consensus. Humans rely on shared facts and good-faith discourse to build political consensus, yet social media environments actively undermine these foundations. As users spend more time online, they tend to cluster in self-reinforcing communities that focus disproportionately on niche grievances, which gradually narrows their understanding of opposing viewpoints. What's more, platform algorithms are engineered to maximize user engagement by prioritizing emotionally charged or provocative content. As a result, users are routinely prompted to express outrage and react impulsively before evaluating evidence. While Kelly correctly points out that social media can foster a sense of community around shared causes, that mobilization frequently intensifies division when groups interact in an atmosphere of heightened antagonism. Paul is ultimately more convincing, because the very mechanism that connects like-minded individuals often separates the broader public.

## My Draft
I agree with Paul's analysis about social media's negative impact on a fragmented society. Humans are political animals by nature, and politics requires some consensus and good faith. With the increase in time spent on social media, people often focus disproportionately on small areas and align with a narrow perspective, so it obviously hinders their understanding of others. What's more, social media's algorithm appeals to users by creating anger and intensity, so people express opinions and act without thinking twice on many issues. While Kelly correctly points out that social media creates a sense of community, that mobilization often makes society more divided than before.
