# Journalism Standards Society

## Prompt (including both student posts)
Dr. Sharma
We've been discussing how people access and consume news in the modern era. Traditional journalism, practiced by established news organizations with trained reporters, has long been the primary source of information for the public. However, the rise of citizen journalism, where ordinary individuals report news through blogs and social media, has challenged this model. Which form of journalism do you think is more valuable to society? Why?

Mia
I think traditional journalism is more valuable because it follows established standards of accuracy and accountability. Professional reporters are trained to verify facts and present balanced coverage. Without these standards, it's easy for misinformation to spread, and people may not know what sources to trust.

Jordan
I believe citizen journalism is incredibly important. It gives a voice to people who are often overlooked by mainstream media and can cover local stories that big news organizations ignore. Citizen journalists also provide real-time reporting during events, offering perspectives that traditional media sometimes misses.

## My Polished Response
While I acknowledge Jordan's point regarding the immediate, micro-level coverage provided by local reporters during unexpected events, I agree with Mia that prioritizing traditional journalism ultimately strengthens democratic discourse by optimizing the predictive reliability of our information infrastructure. The fundamental mechanism at play here is not merely about consumer convenience or the sheer volume of perspectives, but rather about institutional verification protocols and systemic risk mitigation. First, when established media organizations operate under strict professional standards, they do not simply filter content; instead, they decouple public reporting from unchecked rumors and emotional distortion. For instance, when professional journalists conduct multi-source fact-checking before publishing a story on public policy, they drastically reduce the friction costs of misinformation and provide citizens with a dependable baseline of shared facts. Furthermore, relying heavily on Jordan's ideal of unverified citizen reporting without addressing the underlying structural deficit in accountability introduces severe systemic noise into public debate. Therefore, standardizing professional journalistic rigor remains the more decisive factor for long-term societal stability.
