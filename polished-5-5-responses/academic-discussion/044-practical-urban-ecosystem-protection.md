# Practical Urban Ecosystem Protection

## Prompt (including both student posts)
Your professor is teaching a class on environmental science. Write a post responding to the professor’s question.

In your response, you should do the following.

- Express and support your opinion.
- Make a contribution to the discussion in your own words.

An effective response will contain at least 100 words.

Dr. Gupta:

We’ve been discussing the impact of urbanization on global ecosystems. Because of the expansion of cities and their surrounding suburbs, some scientists believe that immediate and drastic measures must be taken to prevent further damage to delicate ecosystems and vulnerable plants and animals living there. Others think that more realistic approaches to preservation are better. Which approach do you think is more effective in combating climate change?

Andrew:

I think we need to act quickly and boldly to protect vulnerable plant and animal populations from urbanization. Urbanization is already destroying ecosystems and causing the extinction of various species, so waiting could make things worse.

Claire:

Urbanization is necessary for growing communities and improving quality of life. If we focus too much on preservation, we risk slowing down the type of progress that helps more people access essential amenities such as housing and clean water.

## My Polished Response
Although Andrew is right that vulnerable ecosystems need urgent protection, I agree with Claire that conservation policies must also accommodate people's basic needs. A practical approach is more likely to produce lasting environmental benefits than restrictions that ignore housing and transportation needs. For example, a blanket ban on new housing within a city could push development farther away, forcing residents into longer car commutes and potentially increasing emissions. A better policy would allow housing near existing public transportation while preventing construction in especially vulnerable habitats. This would address demand for homes without simply moving environmental damage elsewhere. Cities could also dedicate part of their tax revenue to habitat restoration and renewable energy projects, making environmental protection part of urban development. This does not mean postponing action: threatened habitats may need immediate safeguards. However, combining targeted protection with feasible development plans offers a more sustainable way to preserve ecosystems and combat climate change while maintaining public support.
