# Role Art Social Progress

## Prompt
Dr. Gupta:
In our sociology class today, we are discussing the role of art in social progress. Historically, some argue that art—such as protest music, political paintings, or social literature—is a powerful tool that can change society by motivating people to take direct action. However, others contend that art is merely a mirror of social reality; it reflects what is already happening but rarely has the power to awaken people's awareness or spark actual reform. Which perspective do you find more convincing?

Kelly:
I'm convinced that art is a primary driver of social change. It doesn't just entertain; it motivates people to act by creating an emotional connection to an issue.

Andrew:
I have to disagree. I believe art is mostly a reflection of existing social conditions rather than a catalyst for new ones. By the time an artist creates something about a social problem, that problem is already well-known.

## My Polished Response
I agree with Kelly because her claim that art prompts action and drives social change is both historically and politically sound in a democratic society. While Andrew is right that the topics art addresses are largely familiar to the public, impactful art moves people toward change precisely through its nuanced rendering of those events—building directly on what people already think they know. Indeed, a successful work of art is defined by its ability to disrupt complacency and make people realize they do not fully grasp the human reality behind a familiar issue. For instance, protest music or documentary photojournalism often takes a well-known political struggle and gives it a human face, compelling viewers to rethink their assumptions. This updated understanding inevitably drives individuals to take new actions that align with their revised principles. While Andrew's point highlights art's reflective nature, Kelly is ultimately more convincing because that reflection serves as the essential catalyst for reform.
