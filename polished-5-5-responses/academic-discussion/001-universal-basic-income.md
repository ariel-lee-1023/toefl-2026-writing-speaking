# Universal Basic Income

## Prompt
Your professor is teaching a class on economics. Write a post responding to the professor’s question.
In your response, you should do the following.
Express and support your opinion.
Make a contribution to the discussion in your own words.
An effective response will contain at least 100 words.
Dr. Gupta
We’ve been discussing the concept of universal basic income (UBI), where all citizens receive a regular, unconditional sum of money from the government. Some economists believe UBI can reduce poverty and stimulate economic growth, while others argue it may discourage people from working and contributing to society. What are your thoughts on implementing universal basic income? Would it be beneficial or harmful? Please explain.
Claire: I think UBI would be beneficial. It would provide financial security for everyone, especially the most vulnerable. It could help reduce poverty and give people the freedom to pursue education and entrepreneurial activities without the constant stress of financial instability.
Paul: In my opinion, UBI might be harmful. It could reduce the incentive for people to work and contribute to the economy. Additionally, funding such a program could require significant tax increases, which might negatively impact economic growth.

## My Polished Response
I agree with Paul's analysis regarding the potential drawbacks of universal basic income, particularly concerning its negative impact on work incentives and the immense fiscal pressure it would impose on the state. While Claire is correct that guaranteed income could open doors to education or entrepreneurial ventures for individuals who currently lack access, those benefits may be overshadowed by severe macroeconomic consequences. Unconditional financial support without corresponding labor contributions risks lowering overall workforce participation, as individuals might choose to reduce their hours or withdraw entirely once their basic needs are met. This withdrawal directly diminishes economic output while simultaneously requiring a massive expansion of the tax base to fund the program. Ultimately, imposing such a heavy tax burden could stifle the very innovation Claire hopes to encourage, and unstructured cash transfers offer no guarantee of constructive spending. Therefore, implementing a universal basic income would likely introduce more systemic fragility than lasting economic security.

## My Draft

While Claire is correct that UBI could open doors to education or entrepreneurial ventures for individuals who currently lack access, those benefits may be overshadowed by broader economic consequences. First, when basic needs are met unconditionally, many individuals may choose to reduce their working hours or withdraw from the workforce entirely, which directly diminishes economic output and tax revenues. Second, funding such a massive welfare program requires substantial tax increases that could stifle innovation and slow long-term economic growth. Finally, without structured oversight, cash transfers do not guarantee that funds will be spent constructively. Therefore, implementing UBI would likely prove more harmful than beneficial to the broader economy.

- Agreeing closely with Paul without fully integrating your unique contribution into the structural flow of the paragraph.

This eliminates the rigidity that marks the boundary between highly competent and truly fluent writing.
