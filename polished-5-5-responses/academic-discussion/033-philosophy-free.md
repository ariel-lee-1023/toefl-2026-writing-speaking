## Prompt (including both student posts)
Your professor is teaching a class on philosophy. Write a post responding to the professor's question.

In your response, you should do the following:

- Make a contribution to the discussion in your own words.

Dr. Diaz: We've been discussing the concept of free will in philosophy. Some argue that free will is an illusion and that our choices are determined by factors beyond our control, such as genetics and environment. Others believe that individuals have the ability to make free choices regardless of external influences. What are your thoughts on the existence of free will?
Andrew: I believe that free will is an illusion. Our decisions are heavily influenced by factors such as our upbringing, social environment, and biological predispositions. These factors shape our choices, leaving little room for true free will.
Claire: I think individuals have the ability to make free choices. While external influences play a role, people can still exercise their own judgment and take actions that are not predetermined by their circumstances. Free will enables personal responsibility and autonomy.

## My Polished Response
While I acknowledge that our choices are heavily influenced by upbringing, social conditions, and biological predispositions as Andrew noted, I agree with Claire that people can still consciously direct their lives, which proves that free will is authentic. Furthermore, the fundamental mechanism at play here is not whether our decisions are inspired by external factors, but rather how we process our values, build our virtues, and take deliberate action to pursue our interests. For instance, individuals from similarly disadvantaged backgrounds face specific external pressures, but a chosen approach to violate the law is why certain people face imprisonment, not simply because of society or shared circumstances. Therefore, free will evidently exists, and our culture, legal systems, and moral education are designed precisely to guide that free will in good faith, preventing people from harming others and encouraging rational collaboration to benefit our society.
