## My Polished Response
While I acknowledge that modernized infrastructure can yield long-term environmental benefits, I agree with Kelly that limited public funds should prioritize affordable housing and healthcare. Paul is right that smart technologies can improve efficiency, but usefulness alone does not justify government funding. When an upgrade offers a clear financial return, private investors have an incentive to finance it. For example, commercial building owners could install energy-saving sensors and recover the initial cost through lower electricity bills. Cities could therefore encourage private investment in these projects while directing public money toward needs that the market leaves unmet. A family unable to afford basic medical treatment cannot necessarily attract an investor by promising future profits, yet its need is urgent. This does not rule out public investment in green infrastructure, particularly where benefits are widely shared but difficult to monetize. However, when budgets are tight, cities should first protect basic living standards and seek private financing for commercially viable upgrades.
