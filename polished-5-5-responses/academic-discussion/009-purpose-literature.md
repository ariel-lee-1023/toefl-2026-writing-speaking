# Purpose Literature

## Prompt
Dr. Gupta
We have been discussing the purpose of literature. Some writers use stories to reveal inequality or injustice and hope to inspire social change. Other writers focus on memorable characters, imaginative worlds, and entertaining plots. What do you think should be the primary purpose of literature: inspiring change or entertaining readers? Why?

Kelly
Literature can help readers recognize social problems and understand experiences that are different from their own.

Andrew
The main purpose of literature should be enjoyment. Readers are more likely to keep reading when a story is imaginative and entertaining.

## My Polished Response
While Andrew makes a valid point that plot and characterization must be enjoyable to keep a audience engaged, I believe the primary purpose of literature is to deepen our shared understanding of the human experience, which ultimately inspires social change. A compelling story allows readers to step directly into the life of someone whose background, beliefs, or systemic struggles are fundamentally unfamiliar. That deep emotional connection is nearly impossible to cultivate through raw statistics or legal policy alone.

For example, a novel focused on a family facing structural discrimination demonstrates how unfair policies directly restrict everyday decisions regarding schooling, employment, and personal safety. By witnessing these subtle realities through a narrative lens, readers begin to question societal assumptions that previously seemed normal. Literature does not directly enact legislation, but it transforms the public attitudes that render reform possible in the first place.

Andrew is certainly right that entertainment matters; if a book suffers from flat characters or a predictable plot, few people will finish it, and its underlying message will lose all influence. However, compelling storytelling is best understood as the essential mechanism that draws readers into a deeper reflection, rather than the final objective itself. Therefore, truly enduring literature must engage readers while challenging them to view the world from a new perspective, deriving its lasting value from making complex social questions feel personal, urgent, and worthy of discussion.
