# Self Service Print Kiosk

## Prompt
Scenario: a self-service print kiosk using a USB drive

1. Insert your USB drive into the front slot.
2. Next, choose your file from the on-screen list.
3. Set the number of copies you want to print.
4. Select either colour or black and white printing.
5. Choose your paper size before sending the job.
6. You can pay with coins or by tapping your credit card on the payment reader.
7. Remember to remove your USB drive from the slot once your printing is finished.

## My Chunking & Memory Strategy
Short sentences are pure action-plus-place and can be held as one or two chunks. Medium sentences each add one quiet function-word chunk (*of copies, either\ldots or, before sending*). The two long sentences decide the set: sentence 6 splits cleanly at *or by*, then adds a place chunk; sentence 7 is a softened command whose *once* clause is a free time boundary. Reconstruct any missing function word from the chunk type. Never rebuild the sentence from your own grammar; the chunks exist only to keep the speaker’s exact wording long enough to finish.

## My Pronunciation Focus
- Compressed function words: your / the / into / from / on / either / or / before / once / by — each recoverable from its chunk type (place → into/from/on, purpose/time → to/before/once, choice → either/or).
- Word endings (-s / -ed / final t-d): drive, slot, file, list, copies, colour, printing, size, coins, card, reader, printing, finished. Content words that carry no ending (insert, choose, set, select, pay, remove, remember) must stay clean.
- Rhythm & stress: imitate the speaker’s stress on the content words and the slight pause after *or by* or after *once*; do not substitute your own conversational rhythm.
- Content words to say crisply: USB, drive, on-screen, copies, colour, payment, reader, printing — any blur produces the ambiguity the rubric penalizes.
