# Urban Farm Community Garden

## Prompt
Scenario: orientation or training at an urban farm and community garden

1. Welcome to our urban farm and community garden.
2. Water the seedlings gently each morning.
3. Use the wheelbarrow to bring compost from the bin.
4. Pull any weeds you see near the plants.
5. Check the soil with your finger before adding water.
6. You can label each new planting with the wooden tags from the shed.
7. If you find any pests on the leaves, tell a senior gardener right away.

## My Chunking & Memory Strategy
Short sentences are pure action-plus-manner/time or greeting-plus-object and can be held as one or two chunks. Medium sentences each add one quiet function-word chunk (*to bring\ldots from the bin, near the plants, with your finger before*). The two long sentences decide the set: sentence 6 is a softened command whose *with\ldots from\ldots* supplies two free boundaries; sentence 7 is a classic *if\ldots then* whose comma after *leaves* is a free boundary. Reconstruct any missing function word from the chunk type. Never rebuild the sentence from your own grammar; the chunks exist only to keep the speaker’s exact wording long enough to finish.

## My Pronunciation Focus
- Compressed function words: to / our / the / any / with / your / before / from / on / a — each recoverable from its chunk type (place → from/near/on, purpose/manner → to/with, time → each/before).
- Word endings (-s / -ed / final t-d): seedlings, wheelbarrow, compost, weeds, plants, soil, planting, tags, shed, pests, leaves, gardener. Content words that carry no ending (welcome, water, use, bring, pull, check, label, find, tell) must stay clean.
- Rhythm & stress: imitate the speaker’s stress on the content words and the slight pause after the *if* clause or after *with the wooden tags*; do not substitute your own conversational rhythm.
- Content words to say crisply: seedlings, wheelbarrow, compost, weeds, planting, wooden, pests, senior, gardener — any blur produces the ambiguity the rubric penalizes.
