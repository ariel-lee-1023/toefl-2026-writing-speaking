# Library Front Desk

## Prompt
Scenario: a senior librarian training you on library front desk procedures

1. Read every patron at the front desk.
2. Scan their library card on the reader.
3. Use the catalog computer to find each title.
4. Pull requested books from the reserve shelf behind you.
5. Show patrons how to start the self-checkout machine.
6. You can point patrons to the bulletin board for upcoming community events.
7. If a requested book is missing from the reserve shelf, check the return cart first.

## My Chunking & Memory Strategy
Short imperatives in positions one through three rely on direct action-object pairings that require holding the main verb in memory while executing the object noun phrase. Medium sentences in positions four and five introduce compound prepositional phrases that act as natural chunk boundaries. Position six utilizes a modal verb to soften the instruction, where the modal acts as the main cue for a three-chunk delivery. Position seven presents a conditional structure where the initial clause sets up an audible pause, allowing the speaker to isolate the condition chunk from the remaining action and time chunks.

## My Pronunciation Focus
- Compressed function words: prepositions such as on, at, to, from, and for are reconstructed directly by recognizing whether a chunk expresses place or purpose.
- Word endings (-s / -ed / final t-d): specific attention must be paid to requested, books, patrons, and events, while avoiding adding extra sibilants to words like shelf or cart.
- Rhythm & stress: stress falls heavily on primary content verbs and nouns including Read, Scan, Pull, Show, point, catalog, reserve, and bulletin.
- Content words to say crisply: catalog, patrons, reserve, bulletin, and self-checkout must be articulated clearly to prevent intelligibility loss.
