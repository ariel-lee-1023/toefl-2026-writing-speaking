# Hotel Front Desk Check In

## Prompt
Scenario: a hotel front desk during guest check-in

1. then check the guest details on screen.
2. begin by typing the reservation number.
3. Choose an available room from the list to confirm.
4. Open the ID scan tab on the system.
5. then enter the payment details before confirming the booking.
6. You can press the encoding button to activate a new room keycard.
7. For the best experience, you will need to add any special requests when the guest checks in.

## My Chunking & Memory Strategy
Short sentences are pure action-plus-place or action-plus-object and can be held as one or two chunks. Medium sentences each add one quiet function-word chunk (*from the list to confirm, on the system, before confirming*). The two long sentences decide the set: sentence 6 is a softened command whose *to activate* is a free purpose boundary; sentence 7 opens with a front-loaded purpose chunk, then adds an action and a *when* time chunk. Reconstruct any missing function word from the chunk type. Never rebuild the sentence from your own grammar; the chunks exist only to keep the speaker’s exact wording long enough to finish.

## My Pronunciation Focus
- Compressed function words: the / on / by / from / to / before / for / any / when — each recoverable from its chunk type (place → on/from, purpose → to/for, time → before/when).
- Word endings (-s / -ed / final t-d): details, reservation, number, room, list, tab, system, details, booking, button, keycard, experience, requests, guest. Content words that carry no ending (check, begin, typing, choose, open, enter, press, activate, add) must stay clean.
- Rhythm & stress: imitate the speaker’s stress on the content words and the slight pause after the front-loaded *For the best experience* or after *to activate*; do not substitute your own conversational rhythm.
- Content words to say crisply: guest, reservation, available, encoding, keycard, special, requests — any blur produces the ambiguity the rubric penalizes.
