# University Library Resources

## Prompt
Scenario: You are working a part-time job at the university library. You are being trained to help students use the university's library resources. Listen to the manager and repeat what the manager says. Repeat only once.

1. Search for items you need on the computer.
2. The bookshelves are located over here.
3. Our quiet study area has strict rules about noise.
4. The help desk is available to assist with research questions.
5. Printers are available to patrons for document printing.
6. Our workstations can also be used during library hours for online research.
7. When you're finished using them, please return checked out materials promptly.

## My Chunking & Memory Strategy
In item 1, "items you need" is one object unit with an unspoken relative pronoun; no that should be inserted. Item 2 combines the irregular plural bookshelves with the passive are located. Item 3 retains has as part of the main predicate, with "about noise" specifying the rules' topic. Item 5 separates who can use the printers, "to patrons", from their purpose, "for document printing". Item 6 keeps the modal passive "can also be used" intact before the time and purpose phrases. Item 7 begins with a complete When-clause; its remaining action is returning checked out materials, not checking materials out. The original spelling "checked out" is preserved. ## My Pronunciation Focus
- Compressed function words: Search selects for in "Search for items you need"; on introduces the computer medium. "To assist with research questions" contains both the infinitive to and the complement preposition with. In "to patrons", to introduces the recipient, whereas for in "for document printing" introduces purpose. During marks library hours. Preserve you're and them inside the final time clause.
- Word endings (-s / -ed / final t-d): Items, bookshelves, rules, questions, Printers, patrons, workstations, hours, and materials are plural. Located, used, finished, and checked retain their endings, and has retains its final z sound. Need ends in d; quiet, strict, assist, document, and out end in t. Search, area, help, desk, research, library, and return take no added plural or past ending here. Endings require direct attention to the source.
- Rhythm & stress: No audio was supplied, so the speaker's stress pattern and any rhythm substitution cannot be identified. The following are text-based phrasing suggestions only: Keep "can also be used" as one connected predicate, and preserve the complete object "checked out materials" before promptly.
- Content words to say crisply: bookshelves, located, quiet, strict, available, assist, patrons, document, workstations, materials, promptly.
