# Daycare Center Routine

## Prompt
Scenario: A daycare supervisor training you on the daily morning routine.

1. Greet each child at the front sign-in counter.
2. Help children place their coats in the cubbies.
3. Lead them to the tables to start morning activities.
4. Gather everyone in the reading corner for circle time.
5. Wash everyone's hands before snack time begins.
6. You may take the children outside to play once the snack time is finished.
7. For the best afternoon nap, you will need to keep the room dark and quiet.

## My Chunking & Memory Strategy
Focus on holding 2–3 meaningful functional units per sentence rather than memorizing individual words. Sentence 3 pairs a movement action ("Lead them...") with a place chunk ("...to the tables") and a purpose chunk ("...to start morning activities"). Sentence 7 uses a front-loaded purpose chunk ("For the best afternoon nap"), where the natural pause at the comma serves as an anchor before the main command ("you will need to keep the room dark and quiet"). Identifying these chunk boundaries prevents mid-sentence stalling and structural truncation.

## My Pronunciation Focus
- Compressed function words: Prepositions and articles linking spatial and purpose chunks (at *the* counter, in *the* cubbies, to *the* tables, in *the* corner, *for* circle time).
- Word endings (-s / -ed / final t-d): Deliberate focus on possessive *-'s* in *everyone's*, plural *-s* in *tables*, *activities*, and *hands*, third-person *-s* in *begins*, and past participle *-ed* in *finished*.
- Rhythm & stress: Maintain natural speech cadence by stressing key nouns (child, counter, coats, cubbies, tables, corner, nap) while pronouncing function words smoothly and without hesitation.
- Content words to say crisply: *sign-in counter*, *cubbies*, *circle time*, *afternoon nap*.
