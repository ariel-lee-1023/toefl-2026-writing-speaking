# Theater Ushering

## Prompt
Scenario: a lead usher training you on auditorium seating and safety procedures

1. Hand out the program at the box office.
2. Direct families to their seats inside the main hall.
3. Point new guests to the restrooms near the lobby.
4. We greet every guest at the entrance over here.
5. You may guide older guests to seats near the front whenever they need extra help.
6. Follow the marked exit signs and remember that the side doors stay locked during the show.

## My Chunking & Memory Strategy
Short imperatives in positions one through three rely on direct action-object pairings that require holding the main verb in memory while executing the target location phrase. Medium sentences in positions four and five transition into declarative and softened modal commands, using the modal 'may' and condition marker 'whenever' as clear chunk boundaries. Position six uses an and-serial shape paired with a that-clause constraint, where 'and remember that' forms a natural pause point separating the initial instruction from the safety rule that follows.

## My Pronunciation Focus
- Compressed function words: prepositions such as at, to, near, inside, over, and during are reconstructed directly by recognizing whether a chunk expresses place, time, or condition.
- Word endings (-s / -ed / final t-d): specific attention must be paid to families, guests, restrooms, seats, signs, doors, and locked, while ensuring 'guest' remains strictly singular without an added sibilant.
- Rhythm & stress: stress falls heavily on primary content verbs and nouns including Hand, Direct, Point, greet, guide, Follow, remember, program, box office, families, restrooms, doors, and show.
- Content words to say crisply: program, lobby, entrance, auditorium, and exit signs must be articulated clearly to prevent intelligibility loss.
