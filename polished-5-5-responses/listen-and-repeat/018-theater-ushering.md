# Theater Ushering

## Prompt
Scenario: training as an usher at a theater

1. We greet every guest at the entrance over here.
2. Hand out the program at the box office.
3. Direct families to their seats inside the main hall.
4. Point new guests to the restrooms near the lobby.
5. Point out the balcony stairs as shown on the map.
6. You may guide older guests to seats near the front whenever they need extra help.
7. Follow the marked exit signs and remember that the side doors stay locked during the show.

## My Chunking & Memory Strategy
The set progresses from short, bare imperatives to longer, complex instructions. I identified the chunk boundaries by listening for ideas rather than individual words, maintaining a maximum of four chunks per sentence. For the long sentences at the end, I used the cue words "whenever," "and," and "that" to anticipate the grammatical shape, splitting the sixteenth-word sentence into manageable actions and descriptions without altering the original rhythm.

## My Pronunciation Focus
- Compressed function words: the prepositions "at", "to", and "inside", which can be reconstructed from knowing they open place chunks, as well as "and" and "that" in the final sentence.
- Word endings (-s / -ed / final t-d): families, seats, guests, restrooms, stairs, marked, signs, doors, and locked. Words carrying no ending include guest, program, front, and show.
- Rhythm & stress: matching the formal, instructional rhythm of a supervisor giving directions, avoiding the temptation to substitute a more conversational melody.
- Content words to say crisply: box office, balcony, guide, and extra help, all of which are critical to the meaning of the instructions.
