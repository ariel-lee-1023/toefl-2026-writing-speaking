# Lab Equipment Safety Rules

## Prompt
Scenario: A lab instructor guiding students through equipment and safety procedures in a chemistry lab.

1. We keep the larger microscopes over here.
2. Light these burners to heat substances in experiments.
3. Here are test tubes for mixing chemicals.
4. Always wear safety goggles in order to protect your eyes.
5. Follow proper procedures when disposing of your waste.
6. Record your observations accurately in a lab notebook that we provide to students.
7. After each experiment, remember to clean the laboratory equipment thoroughly.

## My Chunking & Memory Strategy
Listen for semantic idea chunks rather than individual words. Short imperatives (sentences 1–3) break cleanly into Action + Place or Action + Purpose. Long sentences (6 and 7) use distinct structural cues: sentence 6 uses a "that" relative description clause to attach the student context, while sentence 7 uses a front-loaded "After" time chunk followed by a comma pause before the main command ("remember to clean"). Holding 2–3 chunks per sentence preserves full meaning without needing to re-invent vocabulary on the fly.

## My Pronunciation Focus
- Compressed function words: "over" in place chunk "over here"; "for" in purpose chunk "for mixing"; "when/of" in time chunk "when disposing of"; "that/we/to" in relative clause "that we provide to students".
- Word endings (-s / -ed / final t-d): "microscopes", "burners", "substances", "experiments", "tubes", "chemicals", "goggles", "eyes", "procedures", "observations", "students". Note that "provide" remains present tense (no -ed) and "equipment" is an uncountable noun (no -s).
- Rhythm & stress: Maintain primary stress on key laboratory nouns: MI-cro-scopes, SUB-stan-ces, CHE-mi-cals, LAB-o-ra-to-ry, e-QUIP-ment.
- Content words to say crisply: "larger", "burners", "substances", "tubes", "goggles", "procedures", "disposing", "observations", "equipment".
