# Photography Studio Setup

## Prompt
Scenario: a lead photographer giving instructions for setting up a studio shoot

1. Begin with the camera mounted on its tripod.
2. Attach the lens to the camera body.
3. Turn on the studio light to warm the bulb.
4. Open the white umbrella beside the studio light.
5. Place the white reflector beside the chair before the shoot starts.
6. You should hang a clean backdrop behind the subject before each portrait session begins.
7. If the memory card is full, swap it for a new one before continuing the shoot.

## My Chunking & Memory Strategy
Short imperatives in positions one through three rely on direct action-object pairings that require holding the main verb in memory while executing the target noun phrase. Medium sentences in positions four and five introduce compound prepositional and temporal phrases that act as natural chunk boundaries. Position six utilizes the modal verb 'should' to soften the instruction, acting as the main cue for a three-chunk delivery. Position seven presents an if-then conditional structure where the initial clause sets up an audible pause, allowing the speaker to isolate the condition chunk from the remaining action and time chunks.

## My Pronunciation Focus
- Compressed function words: prepositions such as with, to, beside, behind, and before are reconstructed directly by recognizing whether a chunk expresses place, purpose, or time.
- Word endings (-s / -ed / final t-d): specific attention must be paid to starts, begins, and is, while avoiding adding extra sibilants to words like tripod, light, bulb, or chair.
- Rhythm & stress: stress falls heavily on primary content verbs and nouns including Begin, Attach, Turn, Open, Place, hang, swap, camera, lens, tripod, and umbrella.
- Content words to say crisply: reflector, umbrella, backdrop, studio, and memory card must be articulated clearly to prevent intelligibility loss.
