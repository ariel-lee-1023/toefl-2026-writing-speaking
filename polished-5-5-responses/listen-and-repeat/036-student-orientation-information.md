# Student Orientation Information

## Prompt
Scenario: You are helping at a student information booth during university orientation. A staff member is training you to give information to new students. Listen to the staff member and repeat what the staff member says. Repeat only once.

1. Welcome to the student information booth.
2. Campus orientation is in the gym.
3. Our cafeteria is known for its tasty meal options.
4. Let us direct you to your assigned dormitory hall.
5. Use the library for research and to focus on your studies.
6. You can visit the student center to learn about clubs and activities.
7. Before your courses begin, take time to explore and get to know the campus.

## My Chunking & Memory Strategy
Items 1 and 2 are a welcome and a location statement, so their actual forms override any expectation of two opening imperatives. Item 3 keeps the phrase "is known for" together conceptually and retains its rather than replacing it with the. Item 4 uses "Let us" to frame an offer of guidance, followed by the destination. Item 5 has two purpose constructions, "for research" and "and to focus on your studies"; their different prepositions belong to different grammatical forms and must not be regularized. Item 6 is a softened instruction using "You can", with the whole learning purpose held as one unit. Item 7 combines a Before-clause with two coordinated actions. "Get to know" is one action expression rather than an invitation to add another chunk. Word counts treat contractions and hyphenated compounds as one word. The length labels are approximate and do not determine an official task position.

## My Pronunciation Focus
- Compressed function words: To the marks the booth destination, while in the marks the gym location. Keep for its after known. To your introduces the assigned hall. The library sentence needs for before the noun research and and to before the verb focus; focus itself takes on your studies. Before introduces the final time clause, and the second to in "get to know" belongs to that expression.
- Word endings (-s / -ed / final t-d): Options, studies, clubs, activities, and courses have plural endings; assigned retains -ed. Known is an irregular participle and should not receive an added -ed. Student, Let, direct, visit, and get end in t. Booth, meal, hall, library, research, center, begin, take, and explore do not gain -s. Campus and focus already contain their final s sounds as part of the base words. The actual sounds, not the chunk types, determine these forms.
- Rhythm & stress: No audio was supplied, so the speaker's stress pattern and any rhythm substitution cannot be identified. The following are text-based phrasing suggestions only: Preserve both purposes after Use the library. In the final item, maintain the Before-clause as an opening unit and carry the second action through "the campus".
- Content words to say crisply: information, booth, orientation, cafeteria, assigned, dormitory, research, studies, activities, courses, campus.
