## Title
Centralization and Decentralization in Pain and Psychology

## TOEFL Domain
Psychology

## Tier
T3 (D≈12)

## Core Thesis
While individuals naturally seek immediate relief by avoiding physical or emotional discomfort, true recovery requires systematic, incremental exposure toward the central source of stress, because avoidance triggers decentralization that diffuses anxiety into broader environmental triggers.

## Pillar A
Patients and individuals experiencing discomfort frequently misinterpret temporary symptom reduction as true recovery -> they adopt compensatory avoidance strategies -> the underlying mechanical or psychological strain expands outward into secondary domains.

## Pillar B
Mechanical Diagnosis and Therapy demonstrates that shifts in pain location outweigh mere intensity changes -> targeted movement brings peripheral pain back toward the spine -> controlled exposure similarly condenses psychological avoidance into specific, manageable triggers.

## Pillar C
Focusing on position centralization over immediate comfort prevents compensatory behavior -> structured incremental confrontation clarifies the exact stressor -> long-term mechanical or psychological resilience is re-established.
