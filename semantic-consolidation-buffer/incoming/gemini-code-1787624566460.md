## Title
Thinking Fast And Slow

## TOEFL Domain
Psychology

## Tier
T2 (D≈6.3)

## Core Thesis
Although humans often perceive themselves as rational economic actors, their decisions are predominantly driven by an effortless but error-prone intuitive system, requiring deliberate activation of analytical thinking to avoid systematic cognitive biases.

## Pillar A
Because the brain's analytical reasoning faculty is inherently lazy and energy-intensive, individuals default to a fast, unconscious intuitive system that frequently falls victim to heuristics like the halo effect, anchoring, and availability biases[cite: 19].

## Pillar B
By consciously slowing down the decision-making process and employing external cues or structured group evaluations—such as conducting a pre-mortem analysis—individuals can forcefully activate their analytical system to audit initial impulses[cite: 19].

## Pillar C
Consequently, acknowledging and mitigating these evolutionary cognitive flaws allows both individuals and organizations to make significantly more rational, objective, and economically sound choices[cite: 19].
