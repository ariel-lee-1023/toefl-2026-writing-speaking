## Title
Residual Judgment Rights and Organizational Authority

## TOEFL Domain
Management Studies

## Tier
T3 (D≈12)

## Core Thesis
While market mechanisms, rule-based systems, and democratic voting handle standardized operations, firm survival requires a centralized authority to hold residual judgment rights when allocating resources under unquantifiable environmental uncertainty.

## Pillar A
Market transactions incur search and negotiation costs, while complete contracts cannot anticipate every future contingency -> organizations cannot resolve complex strategic trade-offs through voting without generating contradictory preferences -> operational paralysis threatens firm stability during novel crises.

## Pillar B
Employment agreements transfer discretion over unspecified scenarios to institutional leadership -> executive authority reserves residual judgment specifically for high-coupling, irreversible strategic choices -> routine, reversible decisions are delegated to frontline specialists.

## Pillar C
Organizational friction decreases as decision rights align with situational risk -> ambiguous strategic options are compressed into a unified institutional commitment -> the enterprise maintains coherence and adaptation under Knightian uncertainty.
