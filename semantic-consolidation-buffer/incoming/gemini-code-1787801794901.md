## Title
The Jevons Paradox and Labor Expansion in Automated Systems

## TOEFL Domain
Economics

## Tier
T3 (D≈12)

## Core Thesis
While technological automation drastically reduces the marginal unit cost of task execution, overall labor demand expands rather than shrinks because reduced operational friction unleashes previously suppressed elastic demand across secondary economic domains.

## Pillar A
Industrial and technological advances automate specific occupational tasks -> public anxiety predicts widespread White-Collar displacement and systemic unemployment -> enterprises initially reduce entry-level recruitment in response to immediate efficiency gains.

## Pillar B
Automation lowers the transaction cost and threshold for resource usage -> cheaper unit execution converts latent market needs into active commercial demand -> the economy reinstates labor into newly created specialized tasks and oversight roles.

## Pillar C
Total workforce employment increases despite task-level replacement -> professional roles pivot from rote execution toward complex judgment, aesthetic audit, and legal accountability -> macro-economic productivity grows through expanded operational capacity.
