## Title
The Post Literate Society

## TOEFL Domain
Sociology

## Tier
T4 (D≈21)

## Core Thesis
Although humans have historically relied on reading and writing as the primary modes of complex communication, the rapid shift toward a secondary oral culture driven by digital media is eroding our capacity for sustained attention, and even though this seems like a mere shift in personal habits, it is fundamentally altering our cognitive structures, political discourse, and educational paradigms.

## Pillar A
The proliferation of short-form digital content and screen-based distractions has crowded out the time previously dedicated to reading long-form texts -> creating a "post-literate" society where text is no longer the dominant medium for information and entertainment -> which leads to a noticeable decline in the general population's reading stamina and abstract comprehension skills.

## Pillar B
Because the human brain adapts based on the biological "use it or lose it" principle, a decrease in deep reading practice reduces our neurological capacity for complex, linear reasoning -> making sustained attention increasingly difficult and uncomfortable -> prompting individuals to continuously seek the immediate dopamine rewards provided by rapid digital context-switching.

## Pillar C
This cognitive shift extends far beyond individual leisure habits, severely impacting educational curricula that now favor short excerpts over full books -> while simultaneously transforming political landscapes by rewarding leaders who utilize simplistic, emotionally resonant, and highly repetitive "pre-literate" communication styles optimized for viral social media distribution.
