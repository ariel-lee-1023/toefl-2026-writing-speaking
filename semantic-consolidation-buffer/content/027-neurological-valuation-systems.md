# 🧠 Active Cognitive Buffer: Neurological Valuation Systems

## 1. Prefrontal Abstraction (The Core Thesis)
* **Core Thesis:** Although human decision-making relies on a unified ventromedial prefrontal cortex and ventral striatum valuation network that calculates choices by weighting situational cues, individual action patterns can be systematically altered by shifting cognitive focus or intentionally restructuring core self-identity markers.

## 2. Associative Evidence Mapping (Logical Architecture)
* **Pillar A (Context/Problem):** Brains evaluate choices through a singular valuation system rather than competing multi-system networks -> subjective option scores fluctuate automatically based on immediate contextual weights and salient cues -> individuals repeatedly execute maladaptive or irrational decisions under immediate pressure despite possessing explicit long-term knowledge.
* **Pillar B (Mechanism/Intervention):** Decision pathways are modified by shifting environmental weights or re-framing available cues -> highlighting present benefits or aligning targets with intrinsic identity markers engages the self-relevance system -> cognitive focus shifts toward desired options without demanding unsustainable executive willpower.
* **Pillar C (Implication/Result):** Adopting a third-person observer perspective creates psychological distance from immediate emotional impulses -> deliberate reinforcement of core values enables external behavioral pruning -> individuals transition from passive followers of social norms to active sculptors of personal and collective behavioral patterns.
