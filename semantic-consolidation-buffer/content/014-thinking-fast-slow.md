# 🧠 Active Cognitive Buffer: Thinking Fast And Slow

## 1. Prefrontal Abstraction (The Core Thesis)
* **Core Thesis:** Although humans often perceive themselves as rational economic actors, their decisions are predominantly driven by an effortless but error-prone intuitive system, requiring deliberate activation of analytical thinking to avoid systematic cognitive biases.

## 2. Associative Evidence Mapping (Logical Architecture)
* **Pillar A (Context/Problem):** Because the brain's analytical reasoning faculty is inherently lazy and energy-intensive, individuals default to a fast, unconscious intuitive system that frequently falls victim to heuristics like the halo effect, anchoring, and availability biases.
* **Pillar B (Mechanism/Intervention):** By consciously slowing down the decision-making process and employing external cues or structured group evaluations—such as conducting a pre-mortem analysis—individuals can forcefully activate their analytical system to audit initial impulses.
* **Pillar C (Implication/Result):** Consequently, acknowledging and mitigating these evolutionary cognitive flaws allows both individuals and organizations to make significantly more rational, objective, and economically sound choices.
