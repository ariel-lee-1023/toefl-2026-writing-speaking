# 🧠 Active Cognitive Buffer: Cognitive Biases And Decision Making

## 1. Prefrontal Abstraction (The Core Thesis)
* **Core Thesis:** Although human cognition is fundamentally constrained by an evolutionary reliance on automatic, emotionally driven heuristics, and even though these cognitive biases persist despite conscious awareness, we can still mitigate their societal impact by designing choice architectures that remove obstacles to rational behavior rather than relying on individual willpower.

## 2. Associative Evidence Mapping (Logical Architecture)
* **Pillar A (Context/Problem):** Evolutionary pressures favored rapid threat detection over statistical reasoning, causing the automatic cognitive mechanism (System 1) to dominate decision-making, which consequently leads to predictable failures in evaluating abstract risks like climate change or large-scale humanitarian crises.
* **Pillar B (Mechanism/Intervention):** Because individuals remain overconfident in their flawed intuitions and are heavily influenced by arbitrary framing, policymakers must employ choice architecture and "nudging"—removing friction from desirable actions instead of applying punitive pressure—which bypasses conscious resistance and seamlessly aligns behavior with optimal outcomes.
* **Pillar C (Implication/Result):** Recognizing the irreconcilable split between the experiencing self and the remembering self reveals that human satisfaction is driven by peak moments and endings rather than objective durations, meaning that public policy must be optimized for how people construct memories rather than merely maximizing moment-to-moment pleasure.
