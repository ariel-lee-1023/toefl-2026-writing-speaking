# 🧠 Active Cognitive Buffer: Linguistic Relativity and Executive Control

## 1. Prefrontal Abstraction (The Core Thesis)
* **Core Thesis:** Although language does not strictly limit the boundary of human thought, acquiring multiple linguistic systems restructures cognitive processing by enhancing executive function, shifting affective decision-making, and altering socio-cultural perception.

## 2. Associative Evidence Mapping (Logical Architecture)
* **Pillar A (Context/Problem):** Monolingual cognitive processing operates within a single linguistic filter, offering limited exposure to varied structural categorizations -> neural networks experience lower demands for cross-linguistic inhibitory control during daily processing -> baseline executive control mechanisms receive less continuous exercise over the lifespan.
* **Pillar B (Mechanism/Intervention):** Parallel activation of multiple languages requires the prefrontal cortex to continuously inhibit non-target linguistic structures and switch cognitive sets -> ongoing neural recruitment increases frontal lobe gray matter density and preserves white matter integrity -> multilingual individuals demonstrate enhanced attentional control and greater cognitive reserve.
* **Pillar C (Implication/Result):** Expanded cognitive control and reduced emotional reactivity in non-native languages enable more analytical decision-making -> cognitive decline and neurodegenerative symptoms are delayed in aging populations -> individuals gain nuanced, multi-perspective frameworks for interpreting global social environments.
