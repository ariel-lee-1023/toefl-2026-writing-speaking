# 🧠 Active Cognitive Buffer: Social Media Refraction and Political Polarization

## 1. Prefrontal Abstraction (The Core Thesis)
* **Core Thesis:** While popular discourse attributes online political polarization to algorithmic filter bubbles, public discourse is primarily distorted by social media acting as a status-magnifying prism that encourages performative extremism to compensate for real-world marginalization.

## 2. Associative Evidence Mapping (Logical Architecture)
* **Pillar A (Context/Problem):** Exposure to opposing political viewpoints on social media platforms fails to moderate user opinions -> cross-ideological visibility heightens intergroup threat perception and triggers identity-defensive posturing -> online participants develop increasingly radicalized stances while moderate voices withdraw from public discourse.
* **Pillar B (Mechanism/Intervention):** Networked digital platforms act as status prisms rather than objective mirrors, rewarding provocative rhetoric with social validation metrics -> individuals facing offline social isolation or diminished status utilize extreme partisan performativity to secure online subcultural prestige -> online political debates shift from substantive policy analysis to antagonistic identity enforcement.
* **Pillar C (Implication/Result):** Stripping social media environments of public engagement metrics and re-anchoring discourse around issue-based, anonymized interactions disrupts extreme feedback loops -> participants decouple self-worth from partisan warfare -> public dialogue reconstructs authentic nuance and reduces societal polarization.
